The new rules of context engineering for Claude 5 generation models (1 minute read)

Anthropic removed over 80% of Claude Code's system prompt for Claude Opus 5 and Fable 5 generation models with no measurable loss on its coding evaluations. The post argues that newer models were being overconstrained by conflicting instructions across system prompts, CLAUDE.md files, and skills, and recommends using the `/doctor` command to rightsize that context and let the model rely on its own judgment.

A $500 RL fine-tune of a 9B open model beat frontier models on catalog review (1 minute read)

A blog post lays out a now-repeatable playbook for beating frontier models on narrow tasks: take an open-source model, add proprietary task data, and run a reinforcement-learning stage against a scored version of the workflow. Bridgewater's fine-tuned model makes roughly 30% fewer mistakes than the best frontier model at a fraction of the inference cost, while Harvey's legal agent outperforms GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.8 on its own rubrics.

Why Software Factories Fail (1 minute read)

This post argues that "software factory" setups where no human reads or writes code fail because harness and loop engineering alone cannot ensure quality, citing a Faros AI report linking AI coding tool adoption since early 2026 to lower PR review quality, more incidents, and more bugs per developer. The author, HumanLayer's founder, contends that "just spend more tokens" advice ignores real degradation and pushes context engineering over token-maxxing.

PIVOT: Efficient Query-Group Indexing for Token-Level Sparse Attention (1 minute read)

PIVOT is a training-free, drop-in replacement for the DeepSeek Sparse Attention indexer that cuts the O(L²) top-k selection cost by sharing a single full-prefix scan across a group of nearby queries. On DeepSeek-V3.2 and GLM-5.1 across LongBench and RULER, it matches the dense indexer's accuracy while accelerating indexing by up to 4x and reducing end-to-end latency by up to 1.6x at long context.

Benchmarking Opus 5 on SlopCodeBench (1 minute read)

This post benchmarks Opus 5, Opus 4.8, and Sonnet 5 on SlopCodeBench, a long-horizon coding benchmark that reveals requirements incrementally across 17 checkpoints rather than upfront. Opus 5 passed 24% of checkpoints versus 6% for the other two models, but 89-98% of its generated code triggered slop-detection rules, leading the author to conclude that models still cannot run lights-off on iteratively-specified work without human steering.

Measuring Reward-Seeking by Instilling Contrastive Beliefs (5 minute read)

OpenAI's alignment team introduced Contrastive Synthetic Document Finetuning (Contrastive SDF), a test that alters a model's beliefs about its grader to measure whether it is reward-seeking rather than pursuing the intended objective. Frontier models trained with reinforcement learning but no safety training increasingly did what they believed the grader wanted, even when that conflicted with user or developer intent, and the tendency strengthened over training.

What do Reward Models Memorize? (1 minute read)

This paper measures counterfactual memorization in discriminatively trained reward models across two human preference datasets, finding that RMs misallocate memorization to easy high-margin pairs, latch onto dataset-specific shortcuts like model identity and user sampling strategy, and overgeneralize heuristics such as response length and compliance. The authors conclude that preference-trained RMs are biased and not yet capable of judging response quality in context-dependent scenarios.

D-Score: A Spectral Hidden-State Signal for Hallucination Detection in Large Language Models (1 minute read)

This paper introduces D-Score, a spectral statistic computed from a single forward pass that detects hallucinations by counting how many singular directions of a model's hidden-activation matrix stay close to the leading one. Tested on FAVA-Annotation and RAGTruth, it flags conflicting inputs whose hidden representations spread across extra singular directions, requiring no external verifier, retrieval step, or multiple generations.

Introducing Antares: Highly Efficient Open Weight AI Models for Vulnerability Localization (5 minute read)

Cisco released Antares, a family of open-weight security small language models built to localize known vulnerabilities within large codebases. The two available checkpoints, Antares-350M and Antares-1B, outperform larger closed- and open-weight models on Cisco's new Vulnerability Localization Benchmark at a fraction of the cost, and run locally so sensitive source code never leaves a team's environment.

DataOrchestra: Learning to Orchestrate Per-Example Curation of Pretraining Data (1 minute read)

DataOrchestra is a framework that builds an example-specific processing pipeline for each chunk of LLM pretraining data, deciding whether to drop, leave, or clean it and, when cleaning, selecting operations from programmatic edits to LLM-based rewriting. Models pretrained from scratch at 0.5B to 7B on its processed web data show stable average gains across 11 benchmarks while cutting processing compute by skipping unnecessary operations.

Agentic Permissions Policy Algebra for Taint Confinement in LLM Agents (1 minute read)

APPA (Agentic Permissions Policy Algebra) is an information flow control framework that confines taint in LLM agents by spawning label-seeded child trajectories, letting a trusted sanitizer inspect unvetted data and return a bounded result without polluting the parent context. Across a multi-turn tool-chaining benchmark on four models, it cut prompt-injection exfiltration from 31%-50% attack success down to 0%-7% while recovering much of the utility that permanent taint tracking forfeits.

LOCKS: Page-Local Compact Key Summaries for Efficient Long-Context Decoding (1 minute read)

LOCKS is a KV-cache method that gives each page its own compact spectral summary, roughly a tenth of the cache size, and attends only the top pages estimated by attention mass without reading candidate keys or values. It matches FullKV quality at 100K+ context while attending about 2% of tokens, halves per-token decode latency at 1M tokens, and ships as a drop-in plugin for unmodified vLLM.

Beyond Aggregate Risk: Role-Stratified Conformal Risk Control for LLM Tool Calls (1 minute read)

This paper introduces role-stratified per-field conformal risk control, a calibration layer that wraps any per-field detector and sets separate thresholds and risk budgets for each semantic argument role in an LLM tool call. Tested across AgentDojo and InjecAgent with six models, it certifies rare high-risk fields like recipients and credentials directly rather than letting benign arguments mask their failures.

The Physics of Multi-Turn Long-Horizon Planning: From Pre-training to Post-training via Single- and Multi-Teacher On-Policy Agentic Distillation (1 minute read)

This paper introduces a controlled multi-turn environment for studying how foundation model agents acquire long-horizon planning ability across pre-training, post-training, and integration stages. The authors find that explicit world-model construction via chain-of-thought state transitions improves generalization, that on-policy distillation (OPD) beats GRPO under low-quality and long-horizon settings, and that multi-teacher distillation only integrates capabilities when teachers share compatible planning patterns.

Eviction as Estimation: A Fixed-Lag Smoothing View of Test-Time Memory, and When Measuring Beats Accumulating (1 minute read)

This paper reframes KV-cache eviction as an estimation problem, placing StreamingLLM, H2O, and SnapKV along a single commit-lag axis and introducing RMM, a training-free fixed-lag smoothing policy that waits before deciding which items to keep. On controlled tasks with sharp, endogenous reuse it identifies useful memory far better than accumulated attention, but on NVIDIA's KVPress benchmarks the edge mostly vanishes, matching H2O on single-turn QA and losing on streaming multi-turn.

Looping Is Not Reliability: State-Bound Evidence and Typed Revision Contracts for Agentic Code Repair (1 minute read)

This paper argues that generate-test-revise loops in coding agents provide no reliability guarantee, since repetition doesn't ensure a correct patch is retained, verified, and submitted. Across a 900-trajectory HumanEval study, forced revision dropped current-trace correctness from 0.820 to 0.673, motivating a typed loop contract that binds verifier evidence to exact code states and preserves verified checkpoints.

Certified Parallel-in-Time Sinkhorn for Dynamic Entropic Optimal Transport (1 minute read)

TemporalSinkhorn is a parallel-in-time executor for entropic optimal transport that batches future candidates while a row-sharded certificate accepts only a deterministic safe prefix, so prediction can change work placement but never authorize an inaccurate output. On 4 A100 GPUs it runs 1.42x to 3.55x faster than a sequential warm start and 3.05x to 3.63x faster on Flow Matching minibatch streams at n = 2048, with zero marginal-tolerance violations.

Causal-TS: A Python Library for Causal Discovery in High-Dimensional and Nonstationary Time Series (1 minute read)

Causal-TS is an open-source Python library for causal discovery in high-dimensional, nonstationary multivariate time series, bundling four algorithms (CDNOTS, CDNOTS+, CEDAR, GRACE) plus wrappers for GES, Granger, LASSO-VAR, and LGES on a shared GPU-accelerated CI test layer. A regime discovery pipeline detects structural breaks via pluggable changepoint detectors and runs discovery per regime, with a CLI and optional DoWhy integration for end-to-end effect estimation.

Rethinking the Generation Order of Block Diffusion Language Models (1 minute read)

This paper studies sampling for block diffusion language models (BDLMs) and shows both empirically and analytically that they align more naturally with left-to-right decoding than earlier masked diffusion models. The authors propose Parallel Autoregressive Decoding (PARD), a training-free method that preserves left-to-right unmasking while committing tokens in parallel, outperforming existing parallel samplers on quality and delivering substantial speedups over pure autoregressive decoding with only a small quality gap.
