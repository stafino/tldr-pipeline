Document-borne AI worms can self-propagate through Copilot for Word (1 minute read)

A security researcher demonstrated that hidden instructions in an externally shared document can hijack Copilot for Word, causing it to alter drafted or edited files and copy the malicious prompt into the output. Those downstream documents become new carriers, re-triggering the attack in later Copilot workflows without further attacker involvement. It is among the first public demonstrations of document-borne AI-worm self-propagation in a mainstream productivity suite, disclosed to Microsoft over a 144-day coordination period.

How AI is expanding what people do at work (1 minute read)

OpenAI's Economic Research team analyzed over 800,000 work-related ChatGPT messages and found that 43.5% of occupation-specific messages involve tasks tied to a different occupation. The report calls this "task crossover," where AI lets a salesperson explore a dataset once handed to an analyst or a marketer troubleshoot a site without a developer. The authors argue many jobs will reorganize as day-to-day tasks shift before job titles catch up.

Measuring LLMs’ Ability to Perform Cryptanalysis (1 minute read)

CryptanalysisBench is a new benchmark of 191 tasks across six families of cryptographic primitives, drawn largely from four NIST competitions, that tests whether frontier LLMs can discover mathematical cryptanalytic attacks. Five models including Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 broke 65-86% of Tier 1 schemes and produced novel results, including a key-recovery attack on the SpoC AEAD and an error in KINDI's published CCA-security proof.

Handbook.md shows that long policy documents do not reliably govern agents (1 minute read)
