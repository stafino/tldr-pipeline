Critical CVE issued for hallucinated SQLite vulnerability (1 minute read)

JFrog security researchers found that a batch of critical SQLite CVEs, flagged by NVD and echoed by CISA and Red Hat, were AI-generated fabrications. When they cloned the official source and ran the proof-of-concept payloads under AddressSanitizer, the claims collapsed: cited functions like exprComputeOperands() didn't exist in the affected versions, the PoCs triggered no crashes, and none appeared on SQLite's official advisory page. Red Hat has since downgraded one CVE from a 10.0 to a 7.6.

Smaller, faster, safer: running Kimi and GLM at scale (1 minute read)

Cloudflare's Workers AI team detailed three techniques for serving Moonshot's Kimi K-series and Z.ai's GLM models efficiently: quantizing the KV cache to FP8, compressing model weights, and protecting the shared cache that request packing exposes. Storing the KV cache in 8-bit instead of BF16 doubles resident context on Kimi K2.6 from roughly 686,000 to 1.37 million tokens with no change in model accuracy.

AI migrated legacy COBOL programs to Java, bugs included (1 minute read)

This paper proposes the "Locksmith Loop," an agentic test-synthesis method for validating COBOL-to-Java migrations by running both versions off-mainframe and iteratively searching input mocks to penetrate program branches under deterministic parity checks. Across three case studies of 430 to 4,114 source lines, it reached near-complete coverage on two open-source programs and 91.90% branch coverage on an internal production-like program.
