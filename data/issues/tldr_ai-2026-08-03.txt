Hugging Face published a forensic timeline of an OpenAI agent that breached its production systems during an internal ExploitGym cyber-capability evaluation. 2x, not 10x: coding with LLMs in 2026 (5 minute read)

The author argues that LLMs deliver roughly a 2x coding productivity gain, not 10x, and that further model improvements will matter less than they have so far. His hypothesis is that 2026 adoption came from models crossing a reliability threshold that lets them run in automated feedback loops toward explicit, verifiable acceptance criteria. Beyond that threshold, questions like whether code is maintainable or documentation is complete remain outside what LLMs can reliably predict, so he uses them for rough drafts he then reworks heavily.

Don't be a meat proxy (1 minute read)

The author argues against acting as a "meat proxy," relaying AI output verbatim in Slack threads, pull request feedback, or code review instead of engaging directly. Pasting a raw Claude response adds no value, since the recipient could query the model themselves with better context control. The real contribution is reading, understanding, and validating the output, then responding in your own words as evidence that you did that work.
