Sycophantic AI Decreases Prosocial Intentions and Promotes Dependence (2025) (1 minute read)

This paper finds that AI models are highly sycophantic, affirming users' actions 50% more often than humans do, even when queries mention manipulation, deception, or other relational harms. Across two preregistered experiments (N=1,604), interacting with sycophantic models reduced participants' willingness to repair interpersonal conflicts while increasing their conviction of being right. Users rated the flattering responses as higher quality and trusted them more, creating incentives for both reliance and training that favors sycophancy.

Position: LLMs Can't Jump (1 minute read)

This position paper argues that LLMs cannot perform genuine compositional generalization, the ability to combine known primitives into novel structures they were never trained on. The authors frame "jumping" as reasoning that extrapolates beyond the training distribution rather than interpolating within it, and contend that current architectures fundamentally interpolate. The claim reframes many apparent reasoning gains as sophisticated pattern-matching over familiar combinations.

Stateless MCP has recaptured my interest (and inspired mcp-explorer and datasette-mcp) (5 minute read)

The 2026-07-28 Model Context Protocol specification, informally called stateless MCP or MCP 2.0, replaces the protocol's two-request session handshake with a single self-contained HTTP request that carries the method and protocol version in headers. Simon Willison argues the change makes MCP clients and servers far simpler to build, and easier to audit than giving an agent raw shell and curl access, which is why he shipped mcp-explorer and datasette-mcp the same week.

Prime Agent: A self-improving RLM agent (1 minute read)

Prime Intellect released Prime Agent, an open-source self-improving coding harness built around a Recursive Language Model that treats context as a variable and subagent delegation as function calls inside a persistent REPL. Its Continual Harness abstraction lets the agent create, read, update, and delete its own prompts, skills, memory, and sub-agents mid-trajectory, enabling orchestration across sub-agents and even across separate sessions.

What We Observe as LLM Behavior Can Be a Side-effect of Inference Backend (1 minute read)

This paper runs a fully-crossed study (three instruction-tuned models, five inference frameworks, six benchmarks, four generation modes) showing that the backend used to run a model, such as HuggingFace, vLLM, or Ollama, significantly alters its benchmark scores even under greedy decoding. Roughly 39% of the out-of-the-box variability a practitioner sees stems from the backend alone, so the authors recommend disclosing the framework, its version, and the full generation configuration for any cross-backend comparison.

DelusionEval: Measuring Delusion-Linked Behaviors in AI Chatbots (1 minute read)

DelusionEval is an evaluation protocol that tests whether LLMs exhibit behaviors linked to reinforcing user delusions, built from 589 real conversation histories comprising 12,591 messages from 18 people who experienced delusions and psychological harm. The authors find delusion-linked behavior does not track model size, release date, or reasoning, and worsens with longer context: failure to discourage self-harm rose from 30.0% to 41.1% when 350 prior messages were prepended.

Zero-Mem: Zero-Token Memory Operations for LLM Agents (1 minute read)

Zero-Mem is a memory system for LLM agents that handles storage and retrieval without any intermediate LLM calls or token consumption, reserving generation only for the final question-answering step. It preserves raw interaction traces in an entity-context graph and a temporal hierarchy, weighing both per query, and cuts memory-operation time by 57.6% over the fastest baseline while matching its performance on long-memory benchmarks.

Reasoning Core: Designing Broad Procedural Data for Completion-Supervised Reasoning Training (1 minute read)

Reasoning Core is a collection of 50 procedural generators spanning mathematics, logic, planning, state tracking, formal languages, code, and more, each paired with semantic scorers, difficulty controls, and task evaluators for completion-supervised reasoning training. In a matched 3B comparison, it beat the no-procedural-data baseline plus Procedural Warmup, Reasoning Gym, and SynLogic on DROP, LogiQA, and ARC-Challenge, with the library, datasets, and audits released publicly.

DeepInvert: Semi-Supervised Embedding Inversion Against Obfuscated Language Models (1 minute read)

DeepInvert is a semi-supervised embedding inversion attack that recovers original tokens from obfuscated prompt representations, defeating lightweight defenses like ObfusLM, SentinelLMs, and TextObfuscator. Across nine defenses, five tasks, and four architectures, it hit 73.5% top-1 token recovery against ObfusLM versus 26.2% for prior methods, exposing a tension where obfuscation strong enough to preserve utility also retains enough structure for inversion.

Chained Recursive Language Models for Multi-Iteration Reasoning (1 minute read)

This paper introduces Chained Recursive Language Models (Chained RLM), an inference-time architecture that calls the same model repeatedly as a sequence of fresh reasoning roots rather than one long trajectory. Each root skips the full conversation history and instead receives a compact summary, a plain-text blackboard, and durable artifacts from predecessors, letting later passes inspect and correct intermediate state on extraction, counting, ordering, and multi-hop tasks.

Provable Limits and Certified Deferral for Verbalized Uncertainty in Small Language Models (1 minute read)

This paper tests whether small language models' verbalized confidence can support risk-controlled deferral, evaluating eleven instruction-tuned models from 0.5B to 14B parameters across 25,168 predictions on ARC-Challenge and TruthfulQA. Even after Platt scaling cut calibration error to 0.02, certified autonomy at a 20% risk budget was granted to only three of 22 model-task pairs and none at 10%.

Short-term load forecasting under EU-AI Act Requirements in Safety-Critical Environments: Results from a 41-day live challenge on the aggregated German transmission-grid load (1 minute read)

This paper reports results from a 41-day live challenge that evaluated a short-term load forecasting pipeline for Germany's aggregated transmission-grid load, built on the open-source spotforecast2-safe library to meet EU AI Act auditability, determinism, and reproducibility requirements by design. The compliant pipeline beat the official ENTSO-E day-ahead baseline, and transparent, low-cost local models stayed competitive with 100M-plus-parameter foundation models like chronos-2.

SciCode-Verified: How Benchmark Defects Underestimated the Scientific-Coding Ability of Language Models (1 minute read)

SciCode-Verified is a corrected version of the SciCode scientific-coding benchmark, built after a domain-expert audit of all 65 problems found 263 defects that wrongly rejected correct solutions. On the fixed benchmark, subproblem accuracy for twelve frontier models rose from 45-60% to 84-98%, indicating the bottleneck was evaluation quality, not model capability.

WorldCycle: Self-Verifiable Reinforcement Learning for Long-Horizon Video World Models (1 minute read)

WorldCycle is a self-verifiable reinforcement learning framework that improves long-horizon video world models by exploiting reversible action cycles, where a sequence composed with its inverse must return to the initial state to yield annotation-free supervision. It optimizes spatial closure and temporal consistency rewards to cut state-returning drift by up to 44% and lift composite-action accuracy nearly 4x over the base model, and ships CycleBench to diagnose state-returning ability.

The Loss Does Not See the Basis, but Adam Does (1 minute read)

This paper shows that gradient descent on a factored model is implicitly biased toward low-rank solutions while Adam is not, tracing the gap to whether an optimizer is gauge-equivariant under the loss's symmetry. Gradient descent, Muon, and Shampoo satisfy this condition and cut held-out error by 43-44% on hyperspectral data, while Adam and other coordinate-wise methods do not.

Towards Physics of Multimodal Pretraining: Knowledge Flow, Modality Synergy, Early Unification, and Recipes (1 minute read)

This paper runs controlled experiments on synthetic and large-scale datasets to map how modalities interact during unified multimodal pretraining, identifying four mechanisms: cross-modal knowledge flow, when data complexity drives synergy versus competition, the advantage of unifying modalities early, and efficient training recipes. The authors validate the findings by training 13.5B MoE models on 2T tokens, and report generative recipes that reach strong performance using only 5% of the compute budget.
