Flock: Fast Proving for Batch Boolean Computations (1 minute read)

Flock is a hash-based SNARK built to speed up the main SNARK bottleneck: proving large batches of standard hash evaluations like SHA-256, Keccak, and BLAKE3. On a single M4 Max core it proves 82k BLAKE3 compressions, 42k SHA-256 compressions, and 30k Keccak permutations per second, and on ten cores it clears 660k BLAKE3 compressions per second. The authors report SHA-256 proving over 9x faster than Binius64, the prior state of the art, and over 500x faster than the fastest elliptic-curve SNARK they measured.

Stablecoin Reserve Requirements Explained (1 minute read)

Stablecoins hold reserves to back every token in circulation, and reserve requirements set the rules for what those assets can be and how they must be managed. This guide walks through the four things every framework covers (composition, segregation, custody, and disclosure) and compares how the US GENIUS Act and the EU's MiCA implement them. Both mandate 1:1 backing in low-risk liquid assets, held separately from corporate funds and reported publicly on a regular basis.

A Hardware Wallet Can Stay Offline and Still Create a Weak Seed (3 minute read) [flag]

Coinkite warned Coldcard users on July 30 to move funds from wallets whose seed phrases were generated on a Mk3 running firmware 4.0.1 or later, after finding the seed-generation path silently fell back to a software RNG instead of the device's hardware true RNG. Staying offline offers no protection, since the weakness lives in the seed itself and an attacker can brute-force candidate seeds on separate hardware.

Stablecoins and Cross-Border Payments: How Digital Dollars Move Across Borders (1 minute read)

Stablecoins settle cross-border payments in three legs: an on-ramp that converts local currency to a dollar-pegged token, an onchain transfer that clears in seconds to minutes, and an off-ramp back to local currency at the destination. This guide walks through the mechanics and trade-offs versus bank remittances, which stall behind compliance checks, multi-party fees, and banking hours. USDT and USDC anchor the market, together holding over 85% of stablecoin supply.

Why liquidity isn't the same as being able to settle (5 minute read)

This thread argues that having ample liquidity is not the same as being able to settle the next obligation, because payments clear against eligible cash sitting in a specific account, not aggregate balances. A bank can hold reserves trapped in the wrong RTGS or FMI structure, own collateral stuck at a custodian, or hold dollars at the wrong correspondent after cut-off. Netting, intraday credit, repo, and FX swaps shrink the operational buffer but never eliminate it, since each still depends on haircuts, credit limits, and operating hours.
