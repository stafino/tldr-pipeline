Coldcard Hardware Wallet Flaw Linked to $70 Million Bitcoin Theft in 41 Minutes (1 minute read)

An attacker drained 1,082.65 BTC worth about $70.2 million from 1,196 Bitcoin addresses in 41 minutes on July 30, a sweep Galaxy Research tied to a firmware flaw in Coinkite's Coldcard hardware wallet that routed seed generation through a weak software PRNG instead of the hardware RNG. Coinkite shipped emergency firmware on July 31, but it cannot repair an existing seed, so owners with exposed devices must generate a new seed and move their coins.

Bitcoin cold-wallet attack spreads to 4,500 addresses as losses near $89 million (5 minute read)

Galaxy Research flagged a third wave of wallet sweeps exploiting weak keys from a March 2021 Coldcard firmware build, bringing total losses to 1,367 bitcoin, nearly $89 million, across 4,585 addresses. Bank of Italy research suggests stablecoins aren't necessarily cheaper for remittances (5 minute read)

Bank of Italy tested 200 USDC transfers across 10 remittance corridors and found total costs ranged from 0.3% to nearly 9% of the amount sent, with blockchain fees a tiny fraction and exchange fees, FX spreads, and local banking charges driving the bulk. How bitcoin cold wallets lost $70 million in an attack that never touched the devices (5 minute read)

Galaxy Research found that weak seed generation, not device access, drained 1,082 BTC (about $70M) from 1,196 Coldcard wallets in a 41-minute window on July 30. An internal build flag told certain firmware to skip the dedicated hardware randomness generator, making supposedly unguessable seeds computationally enumerable and letting an attacker reconstruct private keys entirely offline. Owners cannot reliably tell whether their seeds came from vulnerable firmware, so security firms warn more wallets remain at risk.

Hackers Poison Adform Script to Swap Crypto Wallet Addresses Across Customer Sites (1 minute read)

Attackers compromised a JavaScript file served by ad-tech firm Adform, injecting code that rewrote copied and typed Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Tron wallet addresses to redirect payments to attacker-controlled destinations. Because the poisoned trackpoint-async.js loads across many unrelated client sites, the incident is a supply-chain compromise; Adform detected it on July 27, removed the code, and is telling users to clear cached scripts and verify any address before sending funds.
