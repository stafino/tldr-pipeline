Coldcard Thefts Near $114 Million as Fourth Attack Wave Hits (1 minute read)

Attackers launched a fourth wave of sweeps against Coldcard hardware wallets on Monday, pushing estimated losses to roughly 1,816 BTC, or about $114 million, since Thursday. The thefts trace to a firmware bug that shipped in March 2021 and undercut hardware wallets' core promise that on-device keys cannot be guessed, prompting maker Coinkite to release emergency firmware and halt shipments.

Coldcard Hardware Wallet Flaw Linked to $70 Million Bitcoin Theft in 41 Minutes (1 minute read)

An attacker drained 1,082.65 BTC worth about $70.2 million from 1,196 Bitcoin addresses in 41 minutes on July 30, a sweep Galaxy Research tied to a firmware flaw in Coinkite's Coldcard hardware wallet that routed seed generation through a weak software PRNG instead of the hardware RNG. Coinkite shipped emergency firmware on July 31, but it cannot repair an existing seed, so owners with exposed devices must generate a new seed and move their coins.

Bitcoin Bridge Boltz Halts Swaps Indefinitely, Citing AI-Assisted Attacks (1 minute read)

Boltz, the non-custodial bridge routing swaps between Bitcoin mainchain, Lightning and Liquid, disabled its service indefinitely on Aug. 3, citing months of automated, AI-assisted attacks that outpaced its ability to patch. The bootstrapped team said contained exploits cost the company money but never put user funds at risk given its atomic-swap design, and cut off swap rails behind wallets including Bull Bitcoin and Aqua.

Jameson Lopp says Coldcard exploit exposes limits of Bitcoin’s ‘don’t trust, verify’ mantra (1 minute read)

An ongoing Coldcard exploit has drained over 1,300 BTC (~$83M) from thousands of self-custody addresses across multiple waves, stemming from a 2021 seed-generation bug that reduced entropy and let attackers brute-force affected wallets remotely. Casa co-founder Jameson Lopp argues the incident exposes the limits of "don't trust, verify," since verifying complex hardware and software is infeasible for nearly all users, who inevitably trust vendors, developers, and researchers instead.

'I Did Everything Right': Coldcard Victims Recount Losing Life Savings (1 minute read)

A firmware bug in Coldcard hardware wallets made generated seeds guessable, letting attackers reproduce private keys and sweep roughly 1,816 BTC, worth about $114 million, from more than 5,200 addresses across four coordinated waves. Victims who followed the self-custody playbook, storing offline-generated seeds in safes and safety deposit boxes, are now racing to move remaining coins before attackers reach them first.

Hackers Poison Adform Script to Swap Crypto Wallet Addresses Across Customer Sites (1 minute read)

Attackers compromised a JavaScript file served by ad-tech firm Adform, injecting code that rewrote copied and typed Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Tron wallet addresses to redirect payments to attacker-controlled destinations. Because the poisoned trackpoint-async.js loads across many unrelated client sites, the incident is a supply-chain compromise; Adform detected it on July 27, removed the code, and is telling users to clear cached scripts and verify any address before sending funds.
