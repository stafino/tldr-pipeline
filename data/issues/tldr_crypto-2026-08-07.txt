CryptoJS Weak RNG Behind $5.7 Million in Drains Affects Five Crypto Wallet Apps (1 minute read) [flag]

Coinspect traced the Ill Bloom wallet drains to CryptoJS.lib.WordArray.random(), a 12-year-old function that supplied weak entropy for recovery-phrase generation across five wallet apps, including RRWallet, Bexo, and NanChat. The flaw shrank 128- and 256-bit search spaces to roughly 2^39 and 2^47, small enough to brute-force on ordinary hardware, and on-chain analysis puts confirmed theft at a $5.7M lower bound. Any phrase from an affected version stays guessable even in a hardware wallet, so users must generate a new seed and move funds.

The Trust-Free Aggregation Layer of the Unicity Infrastructure (1 minute read)

This paper describes Unicity's Aggregation Layer, a layer 2 service that lets users transact tokens off-chain while preventing double-spending, using an append-only key-value store periodically certified to a Consensus Layer blockchain. State is digested with Radix Sparse Merkle Trees and proven append-only through an AIR circuit on the Plonky3 STARK toolkit, with no trusted setup. The implementation hits 10,000 insertions per second and millisecond-range verification on a single consumer CPU.

ChainClaw: A Layered Agent Framework for Reliable On-Chain Execution (1 minute read)

ChainClaw is a blockchain-native agent framework that adapts general-purpose LLM agents to on-chain execution, where actions are stateful, adversarial, and economically irreversible. It layers event-driven orchestration, a simulation-based safety pipeline with transaction guards, and an on-chain monitoring runtime, all sharing a cross-layer memory subsystem. The authors report higher safety and task-completion rates than baseline agents across a seven-task benchmark.

Why Bitcoin's BIP-110 refuses to die despite near-zero miner support (5 minute read)

BIP-110, a user-activated soft fork proposing a temporary cap on non-payment data in Bitcoin blocks, is heading toward its Aug. 9 mandatory signaling period despite miner support sitting under 3%, far below the usual 55% threshold. Because it activates through nodes rejecting non-compliant blocks rather than a miner vote, the proposal proceeds regardless. The episode is a live test of whether Bitcoin governance runs on economic coordination among node operators rather than miner consensus.
