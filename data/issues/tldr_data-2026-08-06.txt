From Research Questions to Columns: Operationalization-Aware Data Discovery (1 minute read)

This paper defines operationalization-aware data discovery (OADD), the problem of taking a broad research question and finding which columns can actually measure it, including complementary indicators that never resemble the query. The authors built OADD-Bench from 111 empirical papers, treating published measurements as ground truth for 4,682 question-column labels. The best method, an LLM OADD agent, hit just 0.465 recall, covering under half the target columns and leaving semantic data discovery an open problem.

EdgeLM: Edge Demonstrations for Language Models' Table Understanding (1 minute read)

EdgeLM proposes a demonstration-retrieval framework for table tasks that selects "edge evidence" instead of the most similar examples: nearby rows with different ground-truth labels, plus similar rows the model previously misclassified. The idea is that lookalike demonstrations reinforce the model's default guess, while boundary cases surface the distinctions needed for hard calls. Across five data-wrangling tasks, fifteen datasets, and five LLMs, it lands best or near-best with no retraining or task-specific tuning.

Interview: Qdrant and TurboQuant compression (5 minute read)

Qdrant added a TurboQuant-based compression scheme for embeddings, reshaping vectors before quantizing and layering in corrections that hold recall higher than existing options at the same footprint. At 8x compression it matches scalar quantization and beats binary quantization at every storage class, targeting the RAM-and-disk memory bottleneck that gates RAG, semantic search, and recommendation workloads before compute does.
