Berlin Museum breaks the rules of branding with EIGHTY-ONE different logos (1 minute read)

Berlin's Stadtmuseum rebranded to Berlin Museum with an identity by Stan Hema that rotates through up to 81 wordmarks, pairing two typefaces from a pool of nine and generating a fresh combination on every page refresh. The fonts are drawn from the city itself, from Deutsche Oper station signage to Moskau Grotesk, arguing that a decentralized foundation across multiple locations cannot be reduced to a single recognizable mark.

Dress made of living mycelium can renew and repair itself (1 minute read)

Researchers at the Shenzhen Institutes of Advanced Technology built a wearable textile from living mycelium that self-cleans, near-repairs its own damage, and takes on color or UV protection through plug-and-play add-ons of different fungi and yeast. The engineered living material points toward garments that maintain themselves biologically, though the lab-grown dress remains a proof of concept far from anything wearable at scale.

SOM designs "vertical data center" for Downtown Kansas City (1 minute read)

SOM released designs for 934 Central Street, a 20-storey data center in downtown Kansas City, with a facade configured to read as a conventional office tower rather than a windowless server box. The vertical typology stacks compute on a 0.23-acre urban lot instead of the usual suburban warehouse footprint, a response to land-constrained cities that puts industrial infrastructure behind a street-facing skyscraper skin.
