RTA Studio designs mass-timber groundscraper in Auckland as "continuous ribbon" (1 minute read)

RTA Studio completed an 11,500-square-metre mass-timber office in Auckland, using a diagrid structure of locally grown timber to form a curved, low-rise "continuous ribbon" set within a regenerated wetland. The building consolidates offices and research facilities for appliance brands Fisher & Paykel and Haier Australasia into a single campus, part of a broader shift toward timber-diagrid construction for large-footprint workplaces.

Building Tactile UX: Honoring Intentional Design With Lottie (1 minute read)

Isadora Agency's team built Stress Release, a browser-based squeeze toy, without a physics engine, driving every tactile reaction through Lottie's native state API, DOM manipulation, and distance-based math instead of Matter.js or WebGL. The writeup argues that physics engines produce plausible motion while animators produce intentional motion, and that deterministic frame control (down to a 181-frame mega-squeeze) is the right call when hand-crafted keyframes need to survive the interaction layer.

Has the world been too harsh on Disney's Hexed? (1 minute read)

A FramebyFrame breakdown on X pushes back against the pile-on over Disney's Hexed, arguing the trailer still demonstrates classic animation principles like opposing directional movement, lead-and-follow, and beat-and-hold timing that keep the action readable. The Signal Rail: A Deterministic Motion Grammar for Communicating Conversational Agent State in Terminal Interfaces (1 minute read)

This paper proposes the Signal Rail, a one-row terminal status instrument that gives a conversational agent's motion channel a grammar, mapping states like listening, thinking, and executing to distinct kinetic rules rather than relying on color or a single alive-or-not blink. Its four principles, spatial semantics, one motion rule per state, deterministic golden-frame-testable rendering, and no invented progress, make it a rigorous reference for anyone designing honest loading and status animation.

CSS properties you should know for better text designs (1 minute read)

This piece walks through five CSS properties for typographic effects, including `background-clip: text` for masking images and gradients inside live text and the distinction between `vertical-align` (aligning inline elements to a text baseline) and `align-content` (vertically centering text within a block box). It is a practical reference for designers who want poster-style display type on the web without leaving CSS for image editors.
