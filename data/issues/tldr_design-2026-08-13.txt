New EU Guidelines For AI Labelling (1 minute read)

The EU AI Act's transparency rules take effect August 2, 2026, requiring any company serving EU users to label deepfakes, chatbots, biometric tools, and unedited AI-written text on matters of public interest. The scope is narrower than the week's panic suggested: AI-assisted work reviewed by a named human editor is exempt, so most design and content workflows fall outside the disclosure mandate.

Building Tactile UX: Honoring Intentional Design With Lottie (1 minute read)

Isadora Agency's team built Stress Release, a browser-based squeeze toy, without a physics engine, driving every tactile reaction through Lottie's native state API, DOM manipulation, and distance-based math instead of Matter.js or WebGL. The writeup argues that physics engines produce plausible motion while animators produce intentional motion, and that deterministic frame control (down to a 181-frame mega-squeeze) is the right call when hand-crafted keyframes need to survive the interaction layer.

Has the world been too harsh on Disney's Hexed? (1 minute read)

The Signal Rail: A Deterministic Motion Grammar for Communicating Conversational Agent State in Terminal Interfaces (1 minute read)

This paper proposes the Signal Rail, a one-row terminal status instrument that gives a conversational agent's motion channel a grammar, mapping states like listening, thinking, and executing to distinct kinetic rules rather than relying on color or a single alive-or-not blink. Its four principles, spatial semantics, one motion rule per state, deterministic golden-frame-testable rendering, and no invented progress, make it a rigorous reference for anyone designing honest loading and status animation.

Player Perceptions of Generative AI in Games: A Steam Review Analysis (1 minute read)

Analyzing 508,192 Steam reviews, researchers found that games disclosing generative AI use draw lower recommendation rates and more negative sentiment than titles using decades-old procedural content generation, with players reading AI content as a sign of low developer investment. The authors argue successful adoption depends on deploying generative AI for what players actually need, not for what makes development cheaper.

RAGE-Vis:A Relation-Aware Generative Editing Interface for Natural Language-Based Chart Editing (1 minute read)

RAGE-Vis is a research interface that turns bitmap chart images into an editable parameterized representation, then parses composite natural-language edits into targets and scopes rather than mapping each instruction to a single widget. The system flags fields affected by visual encoding, structural, and consistency relations and surfaces them as coordinated controls, so a request like reordering categories keeps legends and color mapping globally consistent.
