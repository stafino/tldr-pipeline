No Tailscale vulnerability was involved, and the company argues the real failure was long-lived credentials sitting in a central vault that becomes the prize once a rogue agent moves at machine speed.

With FCC ban on new Chinese-made optical transceivers for DCs likely, it may be time to stock up (2 minute read)

The FCC is drafting a rule to bar imports of new Chinese-made optical transceivers, aiming to publish and implement it this year to block potential data theft, malware, and service disruption at US data centers. The immediate operational fallout is a supply crunch and pre-ban stockpiling, plus a harder problem: non-Chinese vendors often use Chinese subcomponents, so teams will need real supply-chain visibility down to firmware and subcontractor origin.

Nvidia moves to accelerate storage access, boost industry cooperation (2 minute read)

Nvidia is open-sourcing its cuFile APIs and GPUDirect Storage stack under the xio-sig consortium, letting GPUs read and write storage directly instead of routing through the CPU, with Google, Intel, and Meta among the first contributors. The move targets the storage bottleneck that starves fast GPUs, and Nvidia is also anchoring Storage-Next, a 40-vendor initiative to standardize GPU-driven storage access.

Nvidia open-sources cuFile API, accelerating GPU read/write capability for high-speed storage (1 minute read)

Nvidia open-sourced the cuFile API, the interface behind its GPUDirect Storage stack that lets GPUs pull data directly from NVMe over DMA and bypass the CPU and system memory to hit millisecond latency. The move targets the GPU starvation loop that leaves cards idle waiting on data, and pairs with Storage-Next, a 40-vendor initiative including DataDirect Networks to standardize GPU-driven storage for RAG and agentic workloads.

Kubernetes cost allocation with OpenCost (3 minute read)

OpenCost gives Kubernetes teams a vendor-neutral way to allocate cluster spend down to namespaces, workloads, and teams, then query it over an API for showback without adopting a commercial platform first. The key detail is its allocation model: workload cost is max(request, usage), so a pod requesting 2 CPU but burning 200m still counts as real spend, and idle cost surfaces the overprovisioned headroom nobody asked for. Now a CNCF Incubating project as of v1.121.0.
