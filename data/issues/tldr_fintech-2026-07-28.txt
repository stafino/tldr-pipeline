South Korea’s KB Kookmin Bank to launch cross-border payment service on JPMorgan’s Kinexys: report (1 minute read)

KB Kookmin Bank, South Korea's largest bank, will launch a blockchain-based cross-border corporate payments service next month on JPMorgan's Kinexys network, becoming the first Korean institution to settle import and export transactions on the rail. The service starts with US dollar transfers across 10 countries through the bank's domestic and Singapore branches, extending Kinexys' near-real-time institutional settlement into Korean trade finance.

X Money is launching in the US starting today (1 minute read)

X launched X Money in the US, a digital wallet with Venmo-style peer-to-peer payments, Apple Wallet support, and a metal Visa card users can brand with their handle. The rollout is limited to Premium and Premium Plus subscribers, advertises up to 6% APY for paying tiers, and follows earlier scrutiny from Senator Elizabeth Warren over consumer and financial-stability risks.

X rolls out X Money to US Premium and Premium+ subscribers, combining a deposit account with up to 6% APY, free instant transfers on X, and a Visa debit card (Zac Hall/9to5Mac) (1 minute read)

X launched X Money for US Premium and Premium+ subscribers, pairing a deposit account paying up to 6% APY with a Visa debit card and free instant peer-to-peer transfers inside the app. The rollout leans on a bank partner for deposits and marks X's first consumer banking product, positioning the platform to compete with Cash App and PayPal on everyday payments.

X Money is finally here, and it’s much more than a digital wallet (1 minute read)

X began rolling out X Money to Premium and Premium+ subscribers in the US, adding in-app peer-to-peer transfers, a deposit account with up to 6% APY and FDIC insurance via a partner-bank sweep, and an X Visa debit card with 3% cashback. The launch pushes Musk's everything-app ambition directly against Venmo, Cash App, and PayPal, with expansion gated on state-by-state regulatory approvals.

Decathlon Germany adds Wero payment option to decathlon.de website (1 minute read)

Decathlon Germany switched on Wero, the European Payments Initiative's account-to-account rail, at decathlon.de on July 20, making Germany the first market in the retailer's footprint to carry the scheme. Wero routes real-time payments directly between bank accounts with in-app biometric authorization, letting Decathlon bypass the 1-2% card interchange paid to Visa and Mastercard while tying the option to its membership program.

Marqeta partners with Zerohash for stablecoin-linked cards (5 minute read)

Marqeta partnered with Zerohash to let any of its customers embed stablecoin-linked cards into new or existing card programs without rebuilding core systems or taking on new regulatory burden. The move gives Marqeta's thousands of live programs a switch-on path to stablecoin spend, but its US model requires a sponsor bank that takes an interchange cut, unlike Puerto Rico-based rival Rain, which issues directly as a Visa principal member.

Intuit launches business credit card that syncs natively with QuickBooks (5 minute read)

The card leans on Intuit's underwriting via QuickBooks financial data, targeting small businesses already inside the accounting platform and tightening its grip on the SMB money-movement stack.

Ramp launches AI token spend controls (5 minute read)

Ramp launched AI Token Spend Management, a dashboard that lets finance teams track and cap AI token and subscription spend across Anthropic, OpenAI, Cursor, and Gemini in one place. The tool ties usage to cost, flags savings and anomalies, and sets limits by team, project, or API key, addressing a category that has grown 20.7x across Ramp customers since June 2025.

Inside the CME and CFTC’s battle over onchain perpetual futures (5 minute read)

CME Group sued the CFTC and chairman Mike Selig over the agency's decision to let Coinbase and Kalshi list onchain perpetual futures, leveraged crypto contracts with no expiration date. A federal court ruling will shape how the US regulates perps, a market that reached a reported $60T in offshore volume last year and threatens CME's long-dated futures franchise.
