BlackRock Private Credit Plans Ramp Up in Challenge to Blackstone, Apollo (1 minute read)

BlackRock is ramping up its private credit operation, using the newly acquired direct-lending firm HPS to compete directly with market leaders Blackstone and Apollo. Visa is cutting 7% of employees in efficiency push as AI reshapes work (1 minute read)

Visa is cutting about 2,600 jobs, or 7% of its workforce, mostly across technology and product operations, as CEO Ryan McInerney redirects spending toward stablecoins, cross-border, and business payments. The cuts reflect AI absorbing technical work like software development, and free up budget for growth areas ahead of Tuesday's earnings, signaling how the largest payments network plans to run leaner.

UK pension providers in talks for £1bn Scale-Up Fund (5 minute read)

A consortium of the UK's largest pension providers, including Railpen and Nest, is in talks with the British Business Bank to launch a £1bn UK Scale-Up Fund dedicated to backing domestic tech scaleups. The move targets Britain's long-standing shortage of late-stage growth capital, which has left many top startups reliant on foreign investors, and follows reports that France is seeking to block UK access to the EU's €5bn Scaleup Europe Fund.

Franklin Templeton Backs CLARITY Act as Wall Street Coalition Grows (1 minute read)

Franklin Templeton, which manages $1.79T in assets, publicly endorsed the CLARITY Act, the crypto market-structure bill that splits oversight between the SEC and CFTC as it moves through the Senate. The endorsement places the firm alongside BlackRock, Fidelity, and Goldman Sachs in a growing institutional coalition, though the Senate has scheduled no floor vote and passage remains uncertain.

BlackRock, Fidelity, other Wall Street giants back the Clarity Act (5 minute read)

BlackRock, Fidelity, Franklin Templeton, Goldman Sachs, and SoFi publicly endorsed the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, which would set a new US crypto framework and redraw how the SEC and CFTC split oversight of the sector. The endorsements expose a Wall Street split, with JPMorgan backing banking-industry changes to stablecoin yield rules that Coinbase opposes, as the Senate races to advance the bill before its August recess.
