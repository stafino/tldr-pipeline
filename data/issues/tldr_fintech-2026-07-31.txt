The Bank for International Settlements ran Project Agorá, a live pilot in which 28 commercial banks including JPMorgan, Citi, UBS, and Deutsche Bank settled roughly $1M in real cross-border payments across six currencies using tokenized central bank reserves and commercial bank deposits. CACHE-UK: A Stability-Aware Memory Editor for Sequentially Updated Quantized LLMs in Finance (1 minute read)

This paper introduces CACHE-UK, a memory-editing framework for keeping 4-bit quantized financial LLMs factually current as regulations, market conditions, and corporate facts change, without the catastrophic forgetting that sinks existing sequential-edit methods under quantization. Tested on a 4-bit OpenLLaMA-3B model over 88,021 UK financial documents, it cut knowledge degradation by 11-17% and lifted generalization to 28%, a 6-point gain over the strongest baseline, though absolute accuracy stays low.

APEX-Accounting (1 minute read)

APEX-Accounting, a closed benchmark from Mercor and Ramp, tests whether frontier models can do real accounting work like reconciling accounts, accruing expenses, and posting transactions across 160 expert-authored tasks. Claude-Fable-5 leads at 56.4% Mean Criteria@3, but no model clears 2.6% on Pass^8, meaning none reliably completes a full task eight times running. For fintech teams eyeing automated bookkeeping, the results show frontier models still fall short of unsupervised ledger work.

Can Large Language Models Execute Parent Orders? (1 minute read)

This paper presents PACE (Plan-Ahead Controlled Execution), the first systematic study of using LLMs for parent-order execution, splitting large orders into smaller ones to cut trading costs without market assumptions or task-specific training. On Shenzhen Stock Exchange Level-1 data, PACE beat TWAP, Almgren-Chriss, and learning-based baselines by 0.65 bps. The authors also found LLMs execute differently from humans: higher model confidence predicted better performance, and the model traded earlier rather than waiting toward the deadline.

Stablecoins and Cross-Border Payments: How Digital Dollars Move Across Borders (1 minute read)

Stablecoins now move dollars across borders in three legs: an on-ramp that converts local currency to USDT or USDC, an onchain transfer that settles in seconds, and an off-ramp back to local currency. The model sidesteps the correspondent-banking delays and multi-party fees that keep a Friday-afternoon New York-to-Mumbai transfer stuck until Monday. USDT and USDC control over 85% of the market, so most cross-border stablecoin flow still rides on dollar-backed reserves.
