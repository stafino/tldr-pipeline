AI financial advice is surprisingly good, especially if you ask right questions (1 minute read)

A new MIT Sloan paper found that AI chatbots like GPT-5.6 and Gemini 3 Flash give financial advice that reliably builds savings buffers for most people over 30, correctly pushing them to diversify, save during working years, and cut stock exposure after 45. The models fell short on adjusting to shocks like unemployment and let portfolios drift instead of rebalancing, with advice quality varying by prompt phrasing and user financial literacy.

SEC to review Nasdaq bitcoin options approval after CME challenge (5 minute read)

The SEC froze Nasdaq PHLX's approval of QBTC cash-settled bitcoin index options to reconsider the decision after CME Group challenged it, arguing that bitcoin is a commodity whose derivatives fall under CFTC jurisdiction, not the SEC's. Bank of Italy research suggests stablecoins aren't necessarily cheaper for remittances (5 minute read)

Bank of Italy tested 200 USDC remittances across 10 corridors and found total costs ran from 0.3% to nearly 9%, with exchange fees, FX spreads, and local banking charges, not blockchain fees, driving the expense. The research concludes stablecoins solve on-chain speed but not the costly "last mile" between crypto and local fiat, undercutting the cheaper-remittances pitch while noting always-on settlement remains a real edge.

U.S. sanctions Iran-linked bitcoin insurance scheme for Strait of Hormuz ships (5 minute read)

The U.S. Treasury sanctioned two Iranian firms, Persian Gulf Marine Insurance Company and HormuzSafe Marine Services Authority, for running a bitcoin-settled maritime insurance scheme that Treasury calls extortion, forcing ships through the Strait of Hormuz to buy coverage and routing proceeds to the IRGC. The OFAC designations expose foreign companies that pay in crypto to secondary sanctions, extending Iran-related enforcement directly into digital-asset settlement rails.
