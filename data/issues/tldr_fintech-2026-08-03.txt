AI financial advice is surprisingly good, especially if you ask right questions (1 minute read)

A new MIT Sloan paper found that AI chatbots like GPT-5.6 and Gemini 3 Flash give financial advice that reliably builds savings buffers for most people over 30, correctly pushing them to diversify, save during working years, and cut stock exposure after 45. The models fell short on adjusting to shocks like unemployment and let portfolios drift instead of rebalancing, with advice quality varying by prompt phrasing and user financial literacy.

SEC to review Nasdaq bitcoin options approval after CME challenge (5 minute read)

The SEC froze Nasdaq PHLX's approval of QBTC cash-settled bitcoin index options to reconsider the decision after CME Group challenged it, arguing that bitcoin is a commodity whose derivatives fall under CFTC jurisdiction, not the SEC's. The outcome will define which regulator governs crypto options, and could force Nasdaq to register as a CFTC-regulated venue or redesign the contracts, with comments due by Aug. 24.

Bank of Italy research suggests stablecoins aren't necessarily cheaper for remittances (5 minute read)

Bank of Italy tested 200 USDC remittances across 10 corridors and found total costs ran from 0.3% to nearly 9%, with exchange fees, FX spreads, and local banking charges, not blockchain fees, driving the expense. The research concludes stablecoins solve on-chain speed but not the costly "last mile" between crypto and local fiat, undercutting the cheaper-remittances pitch while noting always-on settlement remains a real edge.

The billion-dollar question: How will the neolabs actually make money? (5 minute read)

The author examines how neolabs, the new wave of AI-native research startups, will convert frontier models into durable revenue rather than perpetual burn. The piece argues that API access and per-token pricing alone won't cover training costs, pushing labs toward vertical applications, enterprise contracts, and infrastructure lock-in. For fintech operators building on top of these models, the unresolved question is who ultimately captures the margin as inference costs and pricing power shift.
