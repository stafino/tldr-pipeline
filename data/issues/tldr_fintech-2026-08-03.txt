BlackRock expands tokenized cash with new blockchain-based money market offerings (5 minute read)

BlackRock expanded its tokenized cash platform with two new products, BSTBL (a tokenized Ethereum share class of an existing money market fund) and BRSRV (a multichain stablecoin reserve vehicle with daily dividend reinvestment), both designed to qualify as reserve assets for stablecoin issuers under the GENIUS Act. The push extends BlackRock's stablecoin-reserve ambitions beyond the $60B it already manages for Circle, as it moves to become the industry's reserve manager of choice.

Robinhood wins UK crypto registration before new regulatory regime kicks off (5 minute read)

Robinhood's UK arm was added to the FCA's register of cryptoasset firms as of July 31, clearing the regulator's AML requirements and joining over 50 approved companies including Kraken, Ripple, BlackRock, and BNY. The registration positions Robinhood ahead of the UK's comprehensive crypto framework taking force in October 2027, where the authorization window runs from late September to February and already-registered firms have done much of the compliance groundwork.

James Dacombe’s Olix raises at $3.3bn valuation (5 minute read)

Olix, the UK AI-chip startup founded by 25-year-old James Dacombe, raised $312m led by Fundomo at a $3.3bn post-money valuation, roughly tripling its $1bn mark from February and drawing backing from Arm, Hudson River Trading, and Reed Hastings. The company aims to undercut Nvidia on price and speed with chips that skip supply-constrained components, and has hired former Wise CFO Matt Briers ahead of a chip tape-out later this year.

Leopold’s Fall (1 minute read)

Net Interest dissects the collapse of Leopold Aschenbrenner's Situational Awareness fund, which fell 67% in a month after peaking at $45B in assets, up fivefold in three months. The author frames the blowup through Steve Cohen's three risks (liquidity, leverage, and concentration) and notes the death spiral resembled a bank run, with positions publicly tied to the fund attacked as margin calls forced liquidation. The public book was down roughly 85% stripping out private holdings like Anthropic.
