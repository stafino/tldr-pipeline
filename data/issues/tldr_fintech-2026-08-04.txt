Innovations

Wells Fargo joins JPMorgan and Citi in the race to tokenize Wall Street’s settlement rails (5 minute read)

Wells Fargo will launch tokenized deposits this fall for select corporate clients, starting with round-the-clock dollar-to-pound transactions settled on its proprietary blockchain and routed automatically through existing client interfaces. The move puts Wells alongside JPMorgan and Citi in defending deposit bases against stablecoins, keeping funds as insured commercial bank money rather than tokens, with programmable payments and multi-currency expansion planned through 2027.

BlackRock debuts tokenized share classes for select European money market funds with $311 billion in assets (1 minute read)

BlackRock launched Ethereum-based tokenized share classes for select European money market funds, using JPMorgan's Kinexys blockchain to bring roughly $311B in assets on-chain. The move lets institutional holders settle and transfer money market fund shares directly on-chain, extending tokenization from cash-like instruments toward BlackRock's broader push to put traditional assets on programmable rails.

Western Union and Rain Launch Stablecoin Card in 37 Markets (1 minute read)

Western Union and stablecoin issuer Rain launched Stablecard, a Visa secured credit card and digital wallet that lets remittance recipients receive transfers as USDPT and spend the dollar-backed balance at any Visa merchant or ATM. The product is live in 37 markets targeting high-inflation economies, extending Western Union's onchain push, which moved $107.4B in cross-border principal in 2025, into a consumer-facing product.

Wells Fargo to Launch Tokenized Deposits for Corporate Clients This Fall (1 minute read)

Wells Fargo will launch tokenized deposits for corporate and commercial clients this fall, starting with a limited USD-to-GBP exchange and expanding to more currencies and countries through 2027. By putting deposit liabilities on its proprietary blockchain, the bank keeps corporate payment flows on its own balance sheet as stablecoins encroach, using the GENIUS Act's carve-out that excludes ledger-recorded deposits from stablecoin rules.

Announcing Cloudflare Wallets: the programmable wallet for the agentic Internet (1 minute read)

Cloudflare launched Cloudflare Wallets, a programmable stablecoin wallet that gives AI agents a stable identity and a native way to pay for APIs, MCP tools, and content via the x402 micropayment protocol. Account holders can spin up Virtual Wallets for individual agents with guardrails like spending allowances, allow lists, and per-transaction caps, pairing with Cloudflare's Monetization Gateway to build a two-sided market for agentic commerce.

Cloudflare kicks off stablecoin wallet rollout for AI agents to pay for APIs and online content (1 minute read)

Cloudflare began rolling out programmable stablecoin wallets that let AI agents pay for APIs, data and online content, starting with claimable wallet handles and adding funding and payment authorization later. Owners can cap agent spending by approved-merchant list, allowance and transaction size, and the wallets support micropayments through Coinbase's x402 protocol, letting agents buy access without a conventional account, subscription or API key.

Dinari launches tokenized S&P 500 stocks for US self-custody wallets using USDC (1 minute read)

Dinari launched what it says is the first platform letting eligible US investors trade over 700 tokenized S&P 500 stocks with USDC in self-custody wallets, through a partnership with Circle. Each dShare is backed by a corresponding security in qualified custody, though Dinari warned that thin secondary markets for tokenized equities could make positions harder to exit at a desired time or price.

The billion-dollar question: How will the neolabs actually make money? (5 minute read)

The author examines how neolabs, the new wave of AI-native research startups, will convert frontier models into durable revenue rather than perpetual burn. The piece argues that API access and per-token pricing alone won't cover training costs, pushing labs toward vertical applications, enterprise contracts, and infrastructure lock-in. Why Bridge's card program has underperformed despite Stripe backing (5 minute read)

Bridge's stablecoin card program has underperformed expectations despite Stripe's $1.1B acquisition and distribution muscle, the author argues, pointing to thin merchant acceptance and unit economics that stablecoin rails have yet to fix. The gap suggests that owning the infrastructure is not enough. Card programs still depend on interchange, sponsor-bank relationships, and settlement plumbing that a crypto-native rail does not automatically improve.
