5 Interesting Learnings from Stripe at $6.8 Billion in Revenue: 33% Growth, 47% Free Cash Flow Margins, and a $53B Bid for PayPal (1 minute read)

Stripe's 2025 revenue hit $6.8B, up roughly a third year-over-year, its fastest growth since 2021, on a base more than 4x larger. The re-acceleration traces to AI: Stripe processes payments for OpenAI, Anthropic, Cursor, Lovable, and the rest of the AI-native long tail, so its take-rate model inherits their growth without paying their CAC. Free cash flow grew 52% to $3.2B, a 47% margin, making this a Rule of 80 company at $6.8B in revenue while the best public B2B comps run Rule of 30 to 40.

Uber-Gründer sammelt 1,7 Milliarden Dollar für sein Robotik-Startup ein (1 minute read)

Travis Kalanick raised $1.7B for Atoms, his industrial robotics startup, in a round led by Andreessen Horowitz that brings Ben Horowitz onto the board and pulls in Bain Capital, Fifth Wall, and Uber itself. Atoms is the renamed CloudKitchens holding company, now folded together with automation startup Pronto, and Kalanick is aiming it at a "chassis for robots" that makes manufacturing, logistics, and mining more efficient. The pitch is Uber for the physical world, and investors are funding the second act at a scale that assumes the first one wasn't a fluke.

Stripe in Talks to Buy Buzzy AI-Model Marketplace OpenRouter (1 minute read)

Stripe is in talks to acquire OpenRouter, the AI-model marketplace that routes requests across hundreds of models, for around $10B. That would be a roughly 8x markup on the $1.3B valuation OpenRouter carried at its last raise. Stripe already sits in the payment layer for much of the AI economy, and buying the router puts it in the model-selection layer too, closer to where developers decide which model handles each call and what it costs.

Prentis, new AI lab co-founded by Reid Hoffman, Mark Pincus in talks to raise $100M (1 minute read)

Prentis, a new computer-use AI lab co-founded by Reid Hoffman and Mark Pincus, is in talks to raise $100M at a $1B valuation just three months after launching. It has already signed up to $50M in contracts by charging 20% of the savings its agents realize, and claims its 32B Hive model beats GPT-5.4 and Claude Opus 4.6 on two computer-use benchmarks at roughly a tenth of the cost per task. The bet is that automating routine office workflows overtakes coding as AI's biggest use case, in a market already crowded with Anthropic, OpenAI, and Thinking Machines.

Neocloud Fluidstack, which has partnered with Anthropic, announces that it raised an $830M Series A led by Situational Awareness at a $7.5B valuation in January (Maria Deutscher/SiliconANGLE) (1 minute read)

Neocloud Fluidstack disclosed an $830M Series A raised back in January at a $7.5B valuation, led by Situational Awareness, the fund run by Leopold Aschenbrenner. The size and the backer signal how far Series A rounds have drifted from anything the label used to mean, with a partnership to supply Anthropic doing much of the work to justify the price. Capital is concentrating in the few companies that own compute the frontier labs need, and the valuations are being set by AI insiders rather than traditional growth funds.

OpenAI’s early backer Khosla Ventures targets largest fund ever at $5.5B with most going to early-stage AI startups (1 minute read)

Khosla Ventures is raising up to $5.5B across its newest funds, the largest haul in its 20-year history and close to 40% above its last vintage. More than half, $3B, is aimed at seed and early-stage AI startups, the riskiest stage and the one where the firm made its name writing OpenAI's first outside check. The bet doubles down on early AI at a moment when most capital is chasing later, safer rounds, and Khosla's recent deals in legal AI, defense, and robotics show where that money is headed.

Candid Health, which develops tech and AI agents to automate medical billing and claims processing workflows, raised a $120M Series D led by Sixth Street Growth (Lily Mae Lazarus/Fortune) (1 minute read)

Candid Health raised a $120M Series D led by Sixth Street Growth to automate medical billing and claims processing with AI agents. Revenue-cycle work is one of healthcare's largest and most tedious back-office costs, which makes it a natural target for agents that handle repetitive, rules-heavy workflows end to end. The bet is that billing and claims are where AI first pays for itself in healthcare, well before diagnosis or care, because the work is structured, high-volume, and tied directly to how fast providers get paid.

Vertical AI Market Map 2026: Israeli Startups and Funding Rounds (1 minute read)

Israel's H1 2026 funding data is where the long-predicted vertical AI thesis first shows up in the numbers, with billions raised but the more telling signal in where application-layer capital landed. Banks, insurers, defence groups, property managers, pharma firms, and gaming studios are funding startups that underwrite policies, manage loans, discover drugs, and run workflows end to end, not just add an AI interface on top. That is the line between vertical SaaS and vertical AI: the old software helped professionals organize their work, while this generation is being paid to perform it.

Nvidia and SK Group unveil a $500B+ AI initiative that includes an SK Hynix partnership to secure next-gen memory supply for Nvidia and joint development of HBM (Reuters) (1 minute read)

Nvidia and SK Group announced a $500B+ AI initiative anchored by an SK Hynix partnership to lock up next-gen memory supply and jointly develop HBM. The move signals that the AI bottleneck has shifted from chips to the high-bandwidth memory that feeds them, and Nvidia is paying to guarantee its share years ahead. For founders building on GPU compute, it is another sign the frontier is consolidating around whoever controls the supply chain, not just the models.

America’s Open-Model Paradox (1 minute read)

Chinese open models now sit inside most of the American AI stack. Qwen's share of new open-model fine-tunes and adaptations climbed from 1% in January 2024 to 69% by February 2026, and the majority of US AI startups appear to use Chinese open weights somewhere in their pipeline. The deeper problem is legal asymmetry: a Western lab can lawfully post-train on a Chinese open model, but distilling from GPT or Claude outputs is prohibited, so every American frontier advance becomes a teacher for Chinese labs and hands them a recurring structural edge.

The most valuable thing Apple built in the 2000s wasn't the iPod (5 minute read)

iTunes, not the iPod, was Apple's most consequential build of the 2000s. Its 99¢ per-track store launched in April 2003 with about 200,000 songs, hit its six-month sales forecast in six days, and carried more than 70% of legal digital downloads within three years. It worked because Apple stacked strategic levers rather than just selling songs online, and in the process it unbundled the album and set up the streaming and playlist economy we live in now.
