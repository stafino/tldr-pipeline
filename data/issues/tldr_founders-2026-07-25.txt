SK Group Chair Chey Tae Won says Anthropic has asked SK Hynix for supplies to make its own chips, calling it remarkable that an AI developer has chip ambitions (Ian King/Bloomberg) (1 minute read)

Anthropic has asked SK Hynix for memory supplies to build its own chips, a move SK Group Chair Chey Tae Won called remarkable coming from an AI developer rather than a hardware maker. The signal is that frontier labs no longer want to rent compute from Nvidia and the cloud providers, they want to own the silicon underneath their models. If the companies training the biggest models start designing their own chips, the leverage in the AI stack shifts away from the incumbent chipmakers and toward whoever controls the memory supply.

Dear SaaStr: When Should We Start Pushing For Multi-Year Contracts? (1 minute read)

Stop pushing for multi-year contracts in the Age of AI, because shorter deals are now rational buyer behavior, not a negotiating hurdle. ICONIQ's 2026 data shows three-year contracts fell from 28% of new logos in 2023 to 23%, while sub-one-year deals jumped from 4% to 13%, as AI replacement cycles compress every 18 months and buyers avoid locking into a category that could be obsolete by year two. The smart play is to optimize for NRR, get customers to ROI in 60 to 90 days, and earn the extension through results.
