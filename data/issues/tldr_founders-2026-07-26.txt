Google Discloses $94.1B in SpaceX Stock, Marking 6% Stake (1 minute read)

Google disclosed a $94.1B position in SpaceX, a roughly 6% stake that implies a valuation north of $1.5 trillion for a company that is still private. The number reframes what a strategic investor will hold on its balance sheet, and it puts one of the most valuable startups in the world firmly under a public company's roof without an IPO. For founders, the signal is that the private markets can now absorb trillion-dollar outcomes, so the pressure to go public keeps easing while the biggest returns accrue to whoever got in early.

Most accelerators make atartups worse. What do the good ones do? (1 minute read)

Most accelerators leave the startups that join them worse off than if they had never applied. A working paper from NYU Stern's Youn Baek and Deepak Hegde, covering nearly 750,000 US startups across 329 programs, found that 60% to 80% of accelerators drag down funding, growth, and exit odds, while a small group like Y Combinator, Techstars, and Endless Frontier Labs lifts all three by a wide margin. The form of leverage that separates them is knowledge, and the winners teach a repeatable method for reaching product-market fit rather than treating it as a vibe.

Our New AI VP of Finance Closes the Deal, Sends the Invoice, and Chases the Cash. It Took 4 Deals to Train It. (1 minute read)

SaaStr replaced its post-sale finance ops with a single agent by building the function into 10K, the AI VP of Marketing it already ran, rather than spinning up a separate finance agent. Once a contract is signed in PandaDoc, it flips the deal to Closed Won, invoices the right AP contact in bill.com, works the reminder ladder, escalates at seven days past due, and calculates AE commission off cash that actually landed. It took four live deals to train, runs on tools they already paid for, and needs no new system of record.
