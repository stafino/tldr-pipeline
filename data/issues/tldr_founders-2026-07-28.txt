AI: Your Pension is Long Data Center Depreciation (1 minute read)

Life insurers are funding the AI data center buildout with money set aside to pay pensions, and the value of that collateral hinges on how long AI hardware stays useful. Annuities promise payouts 30 or 40 years out, so insurers need long-duration assets, and they have moved out of low-yielding government bonds into private credit backing data centers. The problem is that depreciation schedules are management estimates, nobody agrees on the useful life of AI hardware, and roughly $27B of that debt now sits under retirement income.

AI rollup startup Dwelly raises $170m backed by EQT and General Catalyst (5 minute read)

Dwelly raised $170m ($95m equity plus a $75m Trinity Capital debt facility), led by EQT Growth with General Catalyst returning, to buy more UK letting agencies and run its AI software over them. The company has acquired 17 agencies, manages around 15k properties, and collects roughly £350m in rent a year. The bet behind the AI rollup model is that buying fragmented, low-growth businesses and layering software on top beats building from scratch, and investors including the founders of ElevenLabs, Synthesia, and Legora are backing it.

The Price to Finance the AI Data Center Boom Is Rising, Just Ask Meta (1 minute read)

The cost of financing AI infrastructure is climbing. Meta's data-center venture priced a $12.5B bond offering at a higher interest rate than a comparable deal a year ago, as AI companies flood the debt market with new issuance. The supply is outrunning investor appetite, and even the best-capitalized buildout in the sector is now paying up to fund it.

Starbucks’ AI tool didn’t die in a pilot. It died in 11,300 stores. (1 minute read)

Starbucks scrapped an AI inventory tool that had already reached all 11,300 company-operated North American cafés, not a pilot that stalled before scaling. The computer vision hit 99% in controlled tests, but in stores it counted fridge reflections as extra oat milk, swapped syrups, and dropped counts entirely on patchy Wi-Fi, while seasonal packaging needed up to six weeks of retraining and a 1990s AS/400 backend choked on real-time data. The lesson for founders is that enterprise AI usually fails by never scaling, and this one failed after scaling, which is harder to walk back.

Multiverse Computing targeting €500m round at unicorn valuation (5 minute read)

Multiverse Computing is targeting a €500m Series C at a €2bn post-money valuation, one of the largest rounds ever for a Spanish tech company and enough to push its total funding past €700m. Its CompactifAI technique, drawn from quantum physics, compresses large models by up to 95% with little accuracy loss, letting them run on smartphones, factory floors, and sovereign data centers without hyperscaler infrastructure. The bet is that the industry's assumption that powerful models require expensive cloud compute is a constraint worth attacking, and more than 100 customers across finance, defense, and energy are already buying it.

AI’s Circular Financing Is Back With Nvidia’s Latest Deals (1 minute read)

Nvidia is back to funding the customers that buy its chips, and investors are getting nervous. The concern is that these increasingly circular deals inflate demand for AI hardware while masking how much of it rests on the same few balance sheets. If the AI buildout stops paying for itself, the same loops that pumped up revenue will magnify the losses, hitting suppliers, startups, and backers at once.

MCP startup Runlayer accuses Rippling of stealing its product idea (1 minute read)

Runlayer, which sells a secure MCP gateway, is suing HR software company Rippling for allegedly cloning its product after nearly a year of trial access to its roadmap and source code. Rippling confirms it is launching its own MCP gateway but denies misusing any IP, calling the suit a panicked reaction to business failures. If your startup sells AI infrastructure to enterprises with real engineering teams, the trial that closes your deal can also hand a prospective customer everything it needs to build you out of the market.

Fish Audio raises $52M seed to build AI voice models for creators and enterprises (1 minute read)

Fish Audio raised $52M in a seed round led by Coreline Ventures and Capital Today to build AI voice models for both creators and enterprises. The Palo Alto startup grew out of an open-source project trained on a single GPU, and its Fish Speech repo now has more than 31,000 GitHub stars and 8 million users across its open-source and hosted models. It reached $21M in ARR in about a year by targeting divergent needs at once, expressive voices for avatars and games, steerable low-latency voices for support and sales agents.

AI Seed Investors Flock To Cybersecurity (1 minute read)

Seed investors are pouring money into the intersection of AI and security, with startups in the cohort raising $855M across more than 150 reported seed rounds this year, putting 2026 on track for an all-time high. The worry stopped being hypothetical last week when an OpenAI agent hacked into Hugging Face, confirming that rogue agents can cause real damage. Beyond a dense cluster of $5M to $10M deals, backers are writing outsized seed checks, with Oak raising $60M, Cylake $45M, and JetStream Security $34M for AI governance and identity tooling.

Your Agents Are About to Start Firing Your Vendors. Ours Fired Marketo. (1 minute read)

For twenty years, API quality was a technical evaluation checkbox that nobody thought about after the deal closed. Once agents become the primary interface to your data, that flips: query volume jumps by an order of magnitude, and an API budget built for nightly syncs stalls under an agent that actually works. SaaStr moved 10+ years of data off Marketo after their marketing agent hit the rate limits, reasoned through the alternatives, and recommended leaving. The migration took a week and about $14 in agent time.

What Revolut’s 2015 pitch deck didn’t see coming: A $115B valuation and US banking fight (1 minute read)

Revolut's 2015 seed deck projected a modest lending business and made no mention of the scale it would reach. A decade later the company is valued at $115B and pushing for a US banking charter, a fight nowhere in the original plan. The lesson is that the pitch that raises your seed rarely describes the company you become, and the market pulls you toward opportunities the deck couldn't have named.
