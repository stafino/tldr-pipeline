Software Woes Push Europe Lenders to Seek Rare Repayment Terms (1 minute read)

European lenders are demanding repayment terms rarely seen since the 2008 financial crisis when they extend credit to software companies. The worry is that AI erodes the recurring-revenue model that made software debt look safe, as buyers replace seat-based subscriptions with agents or build in-house. For founders, the signal is that debt is repricing software risk before the public markets do, and the cost of capital for anything sold per-seat is heading up.

PayPal turned down $53bn, and pointed to a beat and a $70 target (1 minute read)

PayPal rejected Stripe and Advent's $53bn buyout offer, saying the $60.50-a-share bid undersells a turnaround still in progress. It used a Q2 beat as cover, with revenue up 5% to $8.68bn and adjusted earnings of $1.38 topping the $1.28 expected, and pointed to Cantor's $70 valuation against a stock trading near $58. The catch is that PayPal never opened a public auction, so it is asking holders to trust the plan rather than the market to price it, from a board owning about 0.6% and a CEO five months into the job.

Why compute might get 10x+ more expensive in coming years (1 minute read)

If Anthropic keeps 10x-ing revenue year over year, it would need to hit $1T by the end of next year while lab compute only 3x-es, and the math only closes if compute gets far more expensive. Spot prices are already up 40%+ from the February trough, and the author argues an H100 running a human-level software engineer should rent for over $250k a year, roughly 15x today's price. The pressure lands on margins, because labs won't concede AI progress has stalled by shifting ever more compute to inference.

Palantir’s New Rivals in Europe Seize the Sovereignty Opening (1 minute read)

European startups are targeting Palantir's government and defense business, pitching sovereignty as the wedge. The bet is that European agencies increasingly want data and analytics software controlled on the continent, not routed through a US vendor exposed to Washington's politics. That opens a real lane for local challengers, but Palantir's entrenched contracts and years of deployed tooling mean the incumbents' switching costs, not the pitch, will decide how much of the market actually moves.

Nvidia and OpenAI in talks for up to $250 billion backstop to fund AI infrastructure plans (1 minute read)

OpenAI is in talks with Nvidia for a backstop of up to $250B that would let it raise construction and lease debt for a 10-gigawatt data center in Ohio on the strength of Nvidia's credit. The campus alone could top $500B, and the chips inside are being negotiated separately. The structure shows how the AI buildout now runs on chipmakers underwriting their biggest customer's balance sheet, a circular financing loop that ties Nvidia's fortunes ever tighter to OpenAI ahead of its filed IPO.

Don't Hit the NOS Too Soon: A Founder's Guide to PR (1 minute read)

Most founders treat press as a distribution channel, expecting a launch to generate coverage that finds them customers. Lester Chen, who has worked with hundreds of a16z speedrun founders, reframes PR as a test: the fastest way to learn whether your story survives contact with someone who has no reason to be nice to you. The harder part is picking the intentional audience, since many founders default to tech Twitter when their actual customers are a narrower slice who have never heard the term "new media.

Building a Startup During War Without VC. And Without Replacing People With AI (1 minute read)

Kiss My Apps bootstrapped from day one and grew into one of Ukraine's largest consumer app companies, past 160 million downloads, without venture money and while building through a war.
