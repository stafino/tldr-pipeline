Multiverse Computing targeting up to $570M in latest round (1 minute read)

Multiverse Computing is raising up to $570M at a $1.7bn valuation, roughly five times its Series B mark and enough to push total funding to around $800m. The Spanish scaleup's CompactifAI tech uses quantum physics to shrink LLMs by 80-95% with little accuracy loss, letting models run on edge devices like phones, drones, and satellites instead of large data centers. The bet is that the cost and energy pressure on AI compute pushes inference toward the edge, and investors including Forgepoint, Santander, and Tikehau are backing compression as the way to get there.

Prentis, new AI lab co-founded by Reid Hoffman, Mark Pincus in talks to raise $100M (1 minute read)

Prentis, a new computer-use AI lab co-founded by Reid Hoffman and Mark Pincus, is in talks to raise $100M at a $1B valuation just three months after launching. It has already signed up to $50M in contracts by charging 20% of the savings its agents realize, and claims its 32B Hive model beats GPT-5.4 and Claude Opus 4.6 on two computer-use benchmarks at roughly a tenth of the cost per task. The bet is that automating routine office workflows overtakes coding as AI's biggest use case, in a market already crowded with Anthropic, OpenAI, and Thinking Machines.

OpenAI Close to Landing $500 Billion Data Center With Backing From Nvidia (1 minute read)

OpenAI is close to a $500B data center project, with Nvidia in talks to provide a $250B financial backstop that would rank among the largest deals of the AI boom. The chip supplier is now underwriting the demand for its own chips, tying its balance sheet to the compute buildout it sells into. For founders, it signals that frontier compute is being financed at sovereign scale, and the capex arms race at the top is now measured in half-trillion-dollar commitments rather than model benchmarks.

British defence startup Agon creating virtual battlefields to combat drone attacks launches, raising $30M (1 minute read)

Agon, founded by a former Anduril director, launched with $30M to build synthetic battlefields where AI-powered autonomous weapons rehearse combat, including counterdrone systems practicing against drone swarms before they face them for real. The round is one of Europe's largest defence seed rounds this year, backed by Lux Capital, Northzone, and Lakestar, whose Klaus Hommels is taking a board seat. The bet is that European defence now wins on iteration speed rather than hardware alone, and the capital is chasing sovereign AI infrastructure that lets allied systems retrain at the pace of the threat.

BAE Systems' energy spinout Nuclear Turbines emerges from stealth with £15M raise (1 minute read)

A BAE Systems spinout, Nuclear Turbines, emerged from stealth with a £15M round led by IQ Capital to build compact modular reactors it claims can generate electricity at one-fifth the cost of today's nuclear plants. The Manchester startup swaps expensive steam turbines for high-temperature jet-engine turbine tech paired with a novel reactor, small enough to sit behind the meter at data centers and industrial sites. The bet lands as the UK, saddled with some of Europe's highest electricity prices, pushes a new nuclear buildout and a "radical reset" of its regulation.

Freehand Raises $75M Series B To Automate Fortune 500 Supply Chain Spend (1 minute read)

Freehand raised $75M in Series B funding, co-led by Battery Ventures and NewRoad Capital Partners, to build autonomous AI agents that manage supply chain spend and back-office operations for large enterprises. The round targets a category Ramp and other spend platforms leave alone: complex procurement and logistics operations, not corporate cards and travel expenses. It lands as tariffs, taxes, and immigration policy break the outsourcing model that ran supply chains for decades, with supply chain and logistics startups pulling in $6.2B across 350 deals in the first half of 2026, the strongest pace since 2022.

A Backlash Against Anthropic Is Brewing in Silicon Valley (1 minute read)

Anthropic has become the most distrusted name in Silicon Valley among the founders and researchers who once championed it, over aggressive competitive tactics, heavy-handed safety guardrails, and its refusal to back open-weight models. The company that positioned itself as the responsible lab is now seen by many builders as the one most willing to compete down the stack and lecture the ecosystem while doing it. The tension matters because Anthropic is also a core supplier, and the same startups souring on it are often building directly on its models.

AMD and Anthropic Sign Major Chips-and-Investment Deal (5 minute read)

AMD and Anthropic signed a chips-and-investment deal that ties one of the industry's largest AI labs to AMD silicon alongside its existing Nvidia and Google TPU capacity. The pact gives AMD a marquee frontier customer to validate its Instinct accelerators against Nvidia's near-monopoly on training compute, while Anthropic diversifies the hardware it depends on to keep scaling. For founders building on Anthropic's models, the signal is that compute supply is fanning out across more vendors, which over time should ease the capacity crunch that has capped access and kept inference prices high.

Nvidia could reportedly backstop $250B loan for new OpenAI data center campus (1 minute read)

Nvidia is reportedly in talks to backstop a $250B loan for a new OpenAI data center campus in Ohio, and that figure only covers the lease and construction, not the chips that could push the project past $500B. The same reporting has Nvidia weighing a separate deal to finance $350B of GPU purchases for OpenAI, so the chipmaker is increasingly funding the demand for its own silicon. The circularity is the story: the vendor bankrolling its largest customer's buildout keeps compute capex flowing but ties Nvidia's balance sheet ever tighter to OpenAI's success.

OpenAI Raised Its Infrastructure Plans to $750 Billion (5 minute read)

OpenAI raised its infrastructure budget to $750 billion through 2030, about 25% more than it projected earlier this year, even as its flagship Stargate project appears to have stalled. The first move is Project Camellia, a $20 billion campus near Savannah drawing at least 3.2 gigawatts from Georgia Power, roughly a third of the utility's newly approved capacity. Most of that power will come from natural gas, more than doubling Georgia Power's gas fleet, which puts OpenAI's compute buildout squarely on the same fossil-fuel dependence and local-grid strain now shaping where every frontier lab can physically expand.

OpenAI unveils Presence, a new platform that lets enterprises launch and manage realtime voice agents and chatbots (5 minute read)

OpenAI announced Presence, an enterprise platform for deploying and managing realtime voice agents and chatbots that answer questions, access company systems, take approved actions, and escalate to humans under company-defined policies. It bundles the guardrails, system connections, evaluations, and update processes that founders usually have to stitch together themselves. It ships through a limited GA with OpenAI deployment engineers, is not self-service, and runs OpenAI models at the core while letting customers connect third-party models for guardrails and tools.

The most valuable thing Apple built in the 2000s wasn't the iPod (5 minute read)

iTunes, not the iPod, was Apple's most consequential build of the 2000s. Its 99¢ per-track store launched in April 2003 with about 200,000 songs, hit its six-month sales forecast in six days, and carried more than 70% of legal digital downloads within three years. It worked because Apple stacked strategic levers rather than just selling songs online, and in the process it unbundled the album and set up the streaming and playlist economy we live in now.
