Banks in Talks to Lend $15 Billion for Anthropic Data Center Backed by Google (1 minute read)

Banks are in talks to lend $15B to finance a 1.6-gigawatt Texas data center for Anthropic, with Google backstopping the deal through power guarantees and lease obligations. The structure matters more than the number: a frontier lab's compute buildout is now being underwritten by a hyperscaler's balance sheet rather than the lab's own. AI infrastructure is increasingly financed like commercial real estate, where long-term leases and creditworthy guarantors unlock debt at a scale equity alone could never reach.

Synthetic-user startup Simile raises $200M at $2B valuation 5 months after $100M Series A (1 minute read)

Simile raised a $200M Series B at a $2B valuation, five months after its $100M Series A, selling simulated users for marketing and product research. The round, led by Greenoaks, is the latest sign that AI unicorns are compounding valuations in quarters, not years, and that synthetic research is drawing the same capital that flooded vibe coding. The founder's premise of accurately simulating all eight billion people is a stretch, since the point of research is that humans are unpredictable, but the money is betting the approximate version is good enough to sell.

Agon, founded by a former Anduril director, launched with $30M to build synthetic battlefields where AI-powered autonomous weapons rehearse combat, including counterdrone systems practicing against drone swarms before they face them for real. Battery Storage Startup Antora Closes $550M Series C In One Of Year’s Largest Cleantech Rounds (1 minute read)

Antora Energy raised $550M in a Series C co-led by G2 Venture Partners and Eclipse, one of cleantech's largest rounds this year and enough to push its total to $770M since 2017. Its thermal batteries store cheap electricity as heat in solid carbon blocks and deliver it around the clock, sidestepping the critical minerals and multi-year timelines that slow conventional storage. The bet is that AI data center demand has turned energy into the binding constraint on industrial growth, and the capital that still flows in an otherwise thin cleantech market is chasing whoever can deploy power fastest.

Freehand Raises $75M Series B To Automate Fortune 500 Supply Chain Spend (1 minute read)

Freehand raised $75M in Series B funding, co-led by Battery Ventures and NewRoad Capital Partners, to build autonomous AI agents that manage supply chain spend and back-office operations for large enterprises. The round targets a category Ramp and other spend platforms leave alone: complex procurement and logistics operations, not corporate cards and travel expenses. It lands as tariffs, taxes, and immigration policy break the outsourcing model that ran supply chains for decades, with supply chain and logistics startups pulling in $6.2B across 350 deals in the first half of 2026, the strongest pace since 2022.

Tesla Weighs Sale of China Business to Pave Way for Potential SpaceX Merger (1 minute read)

Tesla is weighing a sale or spinoff of its China business, its second-largest market, over geopolitical concerns and to clear the path for a possible SpaceX merger. Shedding China would cut Tesla's exposure to its biggest manufacturing base and a growing share of its deliveries, a steep price to make the corporate structure clean enough to fold in SpaceX. For founders, it is a reminder that at a certain scale, geopolitical risk and deal architecture start dictating strategy more than the core product does.

A Backlash Against Anthropic Is Brewing in Silicon Valley (1 minute read)

Anthropic has become the most distrusted name in Silicon Valley among the founders and researchers who once championed it, over aggressive competitive tactics, heavy-handed safety guardrails, and its refusal to back open-weight models. The company that positioned itself as the responsible lab is now seen by many builders as the one most willing to compete down the stack and lecture the ecosystem while doing it. The tension matters because Anthropic is also a core supplier, and the same startups souring on it are often building directly on its models.

Five Takeaways From the Times Investigation Into Larry Ellison’s A.I. Gamble (1 minute read)

Oracle has taken on enormous debt to build AI data centers around the world, a bet that now stretches well beyond one company's balance sheet. The Times investigation frames Larry Ellison's spending as a leveraged wager that the AI compute buildout will pay off before the loans come due. If the returns lag the capex, the risk doesn't stay contained at Oracle, because the same debt-funded data-center playbook is now underwriting a large share of the industry's growth.

AWS is ‘booming,’ but Amazon’s free cash flow turns negative on record AI spending (1 minute read)

Amazon's AWS grew 37% last quarter, its fastest since 2021, yet the company's trailing free cash flow just turned negative for the first time since 2023 as AI capex outran even booming cloud profits. Amazon spent a net $169 billion on property and equipment, up $66 billion year over year, and Jassy raised the 2026 cash capex forecast to about $220 billion. The signal for founders building on the hyperscalers is that your infrastructure supplier is now torching its own free cash flow to hold the frontier, and that spending sets the price floor under everything you rent.

An interview with Granola CEO Chris Pedregal on making the AI note taker invisible and work across apps, and why he rejects executives seeking employees' notes (Casey Newton/Platformer) (1 minute read)

Granola CEO Chris Pedregal is betting that the best AI note-taker is the one you forget is running, working invisibly across whatever apps you already use rather than as a bot that joins the call. He also draws a hard line against executives who ask to see their employees' notes, treating private capture as the product's foundation rather than a feature to trade away. The stance is a wedge against enterprise buyers who want surveillance, and it positions trust as the moat in a category where every incumbent is bolting on AI transcription.

Amazon hikes 2026 capex to $220 billion due to higher memory costs (1 minute read)

Amazon raised its 2026 capital spending forecast to $220B, up from the $200B it held to in April, with CEO Andy Jassy blaming rising memory prices and saying even that won't cover AI demand. AWS grew 37% year over year, its fastest since 2021, and the AI and homegrown-chips units each cleared a $25B run rate. Jassy said the company already sees striking demand for 2028, which means the hyperscaler capex race isn't a one-year spike but a multi-year commitment that keeps pulling compute, memory, and pricing out of reach for everyone downstream.

Nexus Data Centers in advanced talks to secure $15B for Google-backed Anthropic data center (1 minute read)

Nexus Data Centers is in advanced talks to borrow $15B, with Morgan Stanley leading, to build an Anthropic AI campus in Hubbard, Texas. It is the latest in a run of infrastructure deals Anthropic has struck this year with AMD, SpaceX, and Broadcom, on top of Google's commitment to invest up to $40B, and it shows frontier labs now compete as much on financeable capacity as on model quality.

Amazon's AWS posts fastest growth since 2021, citing AI and chip demand (1 minute read)

AWS grew almost 37% in Q2 to $42.23B, its fastest expansion since 2021, with the AI business and its custom chips each now running above $25B in annualized revenue. AWS still throws off nearly 61% of Amazon's operating profit at a 36.8% margin, but Google Cloud grew 82% and Azure 43% off smaller bases, so the pace gap is where investors are watching for share to shift. The tell is capex, up 68% to $54.21B, as every hyperscaler races to stand up AI data centers customers are already committing to for years.

Amazon Shares Jump as Cloud Sales—and Spending—Accelerate (1 minute read)

AWS grew 37% in the second quarter, and Amazon raised its capital spending forecast to keep the AI buildout going, sending the stock higher. The message from the cloud giants is now consistent: demand for AI compute is outrunning supply, and the winners are the ones willing to spend the most to close the gap. For founders, the takeaway is that infrastructure prices are unlikely to fall soon, because the companies you rent from are pouring their record profits back into capex rather than passing savings down.

Meta Stumbles as Tech Investors Demand Better Answers on AI Spending (1 minute read)

Meta and Microsoft both spent enormous sums on AI, but their stocks split hard after earnings because investors could see where one company's money was going and not the other's. Microsoft rallied by tying its capex to Azure demand and cloud revenue it could point to. Meta fell because it kept raising its spending guidance without a clear line to the revenue paying for it. The bar has shifted from how much you invest in AI to how legibly you can show it turning into money.

VC is changing dramatically — what’s a founder to do? (1 minute read)

Venture capital has never had more money and never been harder to raise, with US firms deploying $412.7 billion in the first half of 2026 alone while 33% of all dollars went to the top 1% of companies by valuation. The bar moved with it, as the median revenue needed for a Series A roughly tripled to about $3.5M in ARR and only 15.4% of the 2022 seed cohort reached a Series A within two years. Unless you are building foundational AI, the smart play is to treat VC as one option and build a real plan around alternative financing and growth.

The Cold Email (1 minute read)

Cold outreach still works, and the author credits three of the biggest turns in his life to it: an extra next-day-air essay that pulled him off a Carnegie Mellon waitlist that only clears 1% to 10% of applicants, a cold email answering a GitHub tweet that landed him a job on the spot, and a Twitter DM that eventually made him part-owner of a Serie A soccer club. The pattern is that a low-cost, low-risk ask often costs the recipient almost nothing to say yes to. The reason to send it is simple: it rarely hurts, and occasionally it changes everything.

Dear SaaStr:  How Do You Steal Customers From the Leader in the Space? (1 minute read)

Stealing customers from the market leader starts with showing up exactly where they are, not carving out your own quiet corner. Roughly 80% of buyers pick the top brand, but the rest go shopping at the same events, podcasts, and newsletters, so being the clear #2 gets you invited when a renewal goes bad. Then win on the switching cost itself: drip-market every lost deal, offer to buy out the remaining term of a competitor's contract, and do the migration work for them at no charge.

The Hidden Math of Behavioral Health (1 minute read)

Behavioral health clinics don't fail for lack of demand, they fail on reimbursement. Camber's data shows the same insurer paying a clinic more than double for an identical service in one state versus another, with high-low variance running from 1.35X to 2.09X across four insurers. In a business with thin margins and young practices, getting paid in full and on time is what separates a clinic that stays open from one that closes and cuts a child off from therapy.

The One Hire You [Probably] Just Can’t Make: Chief Product & Technology Officer (1 minute read)

The CPTO title is spreading at B2B companies past $100M ARR, especially ones trying to get more agentic, but the role almost always collapses because the person is neither a real product leader nor a real technology leader. They don't ship code or review PRs, and they aren't in customer calls every day either. The work still needs two different humans: a VP of Engineering technical enough to make architecture calls in the AI era, and a Head of Product who lives inside the customer's problem.

Founder Traits And One Big AI Test: How Former NEA Partner Vanessa Larco Picks Winners (1 minute read)

Larco backs founder potential over the pitch, betting that a strong technical team will find the right idea faster than a mediocre one will execute a great one. The wrapper looks like a product until the model it depends on ships the same feature natively.
