VC-backed startups commit more fraud, and researchers think they know why (1 minute read)

Two new studies, from Imperial College and Emlyon and separately from the University of Toronto, find that VC-backed startups are more likely to face securities fraud charges than companies that never raised venture money, and that ventures launched in overheated markets with weak due diligence are 19% more likely to later commit fraud. The mechanism is pressure: when the gap between investor expectations and real performance grows, founders start "façading," escalating from lies to fabricated evidence. The authors point to today's frothy AI environment as exactly the conditions that push founders across the line.

Chinese VC firms are rushing to raise new funds after three years of record-low fundraising, amid renewed enthusiasm for China's tech, AI, and robotics sectors (Eleanor Olcott/Financial Times) (1 minute read)

Chinese VC firms are racing to raise new funds after three years of record-low fundraising, driven by renewed enthusiasm for the country's tech, AI, and robotics sectors. Dear SaaStr:  How Do You Compensate Reps on Multi-Year Deals? (1 minute read)

Commission plans quietly decide how your reps behave on multi-year deals, and the safe default early on is to pay full commission only on cash collected up front, since cash is king and future years shouldn't count as bookings. Fewer than 10% of startups pay 100% on prepaid Year 2 and 3, so consider dropping to a 25% commission on those years past $10M ARR and guarding hard against discounting. When SaaStr's own plan was later loosened, one rep gave away a $200k lifetime deal, proving incentives set the ceiling on how badly a deal can go.

Dear SaaStr:  I Fired My VP of Sales.  What Now? (1 minute read)

Firing your VP of Sales feels like a crisis, but the fix is usually a plan, not another quick hire. Step in as interim yourself or back a strong Director or AE who is a stretch, because you know the product and customers better than any replacement will on day one. Then audit revenue per lead, close rates, and cycle length to find what was actually broken before you hire again. A bad VP hire can set you back a year, so keep the pipeline moving and take the time to find the right fit.

When a 3.8x “Exit” Becomes 1.6x: The Gap Between Markups and Returns (1 minute read)

A 3.8x markup on a marquee valuation can land under 1.6x in the bank, and the gap is one of the least understood dynamics in venture. The press-release price is the gross that nobody gets paid: cash, debt, taxes, working capital, escrow, and transaction costs all come off the top first. But dilution is the silent multiplier killer, because every round after your entry shrinks your ownership, so a company can more than triple in value while your stake quietly halves.
