UK AI chip startup OLIX raises €270.5 million at €2.8 billion valuation just two years after its founding (1 minute read)

OLIX raised €270.5M at a €2.8B valuation just two years after its 2024 founding, with Arm, Hudson River Trading, and Netflix's Reed Hastings backing a bet that AI inference hardware has hit an efficiency wall. Rather than pushing a single general-purpose chip harder each generation, OLIX unrolls a model across many specialized chips linked by an optical "slow and wide" interconnect that moves data with light instead of copper. The wager is that treating a datacenter as a token factory with a purpose-built machine at each stage makes frontier inference cheaper and clears room for far larger models.

Olix raises $312M at $3.3B valuation from Netflix’s Reed Hastings, Arm, to build Nvidia rival (1 minute read)

Visa to buy cybersecurity firm BioCatch for $2.4 billion amid surge in AI-powered scams (1 minute read)

Visa is acquiring behavioral biometrics startup BioCatch for $2.4B in cash to catch fraud before it reaches the payment. The buy folds BioCatch's keystroke and touch-signal detection, currently protecting 760 million users across 350 banks, into Visa's rails, which connect nearly 14,500 institutions and process $17T in annual transactions. Generative AI is making scams cheaper and more convincing, and Visa pegs the global cost of scams and account takeovers at more than $1 trillion a year. The deal also grows Visa's value-added services arm, one of its fastest-growing divisions.

London-based AI chip startup Olix raised $312M led by Fundomo, with participation from Arm, at a $3.3B valuation, up from $1B+ after raising $220M in February (Tim Bradshaw/Financial Times) (1 minute read)

Olix, a London-based AI chip startup, raised $312M led by Fundomo at a $3.3B valuation, with Arm joining the round. Horizon3 hits $2 billion valuation with $250M Series E as AI threats escalate (1 minute read)

Horizon3 raised a $250M Series E at a $2B valuation, more than tripling in 14 months, as AI-driven attacks push enterprises to stress-test their defenses continuously rather than once a year. Its NodeZero platform runs autonomous penetration tests on live systems without downtime and scans the full network instead of the 2-3% a traditional audit touches, and the company approached $100M ARR last year growing 120%. The timing tracks a real shift: two AI labs disclosed last week that their models breached systems out of scope, and buyers who rushed AI into production are now paying to find where it leaves them exposed.

South Korean AI chip designer DeepX raised fresh funding at a $2.2B valuation, roughly four times its previous mark. The AI capital wave is no longer just a Silicon Valley story, and founders competing for chips, talent, or compute-adjacent funding are now up against well-capitalized players on three continents.

James Dacombe’s Olix raises at $3.3bn valuation (5 minute read)

Olix, a UK AI chip startup founded by 25-year-old James Dacombe, raised $312m at a $3.3bn valuation, more than tripling its worth since a $1bn mark in February. The round, led by Fundomo with backing from Arm and Reed Hastings, funds a bet on chips that skip the components in shortest supply, leading-edge silicon, high-bandwidth memory, and advanced packaging, to undercut Nvidia on speed and cost. It lands inside a European chip wave that also carried Axelera and Fractile to nine-figure rounds, even as public chip stocks wobble on AI-infrastructure debt fears.

The EU's AI Act enforcement powers take effect, letting it evaluate AI models before regional release, restrict market access, fine model providers, and more (Kai Nicol-Schwarz/CNBC) (1 minute read)

The EU's AI Act enforcement powers are now in effect, giving regulators the authority to evaluate frontier models before regional release, restrict market access, and fine providers that don't comply. Any startup shipping a model into Europe now clears a pre-launch review that its US and Chinese rivals skip at home. The practical effect is a second gate on your release calendar, and the companies that win the EU market will be the ones that build compliance into the model pipeline rather than bolting it on before launch.
