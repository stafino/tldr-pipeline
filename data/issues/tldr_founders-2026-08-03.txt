UK AI chip startup OLIX raises €270.5 million at €2.8 billion valuation just two years after its founding (1 minute read)

OLIX raised €270.5M at a €2.8B valuation just two years after its 2024 founding, with Arm, Hudson River Trading, and Netflix's Reed Hastings backing a bet that AI inference hardware has hit an efficiency wall. Rather than pushing a single general-purpose chip harder each generation, OLIX unrolls a model across many specialized chips linked by an optical "slow and wide" interconnect that moves data with light instead of copper. The wager is that treating a datacenter as a token factory with a purpose-built machine at each stage makes frontier inference cheaper and clears room for far larger models.

Visa to buy cybersecurity firm BioCatch for $2.4 billion amid surge in AI-powered scams (1 minute read)

Visa is acquiring behavioral biometrics startup BioCatch for $2.4B in cash to catch fraud before it reaches the payment. The buy folds BioCatch's keystroke and touch-signal detection, currently protecting 760 million users across 350 banks, into Visa's rails, which connect nearly 14,500 institutions and process $17T in annual transactions. Generative AI is making scams cheaper and more convincing, and Visa pegs the global cost of scams and account takeovers at more than $1 trillion a year. The deal also grows Visa's value-added services arm, one of its fastest-growing divisions.

Horizon3 hits $2 billion valuation with $250M Series E as AI threats escalate (1 minute read)

Horizon3 raised a $250M Series E at a $2B valuation, more than tripling in 14 months, as AI-driven attacks push enterprises to stress-test their defenses continuously rather than once a year. Its NodeZero platform runs autonomous penetration tests on live systems without downtime and scans the full network instead of the 2-3% a traditional audit touches, and the company approached $100M ARR last year growing 120%. The timing tracks a real shift: two AI labs disclosed last week that their models breached systems out of scope, and buyers who rushed AI into production are now paying to find where it leaves them exposed.

The SaaStr author spent years believing the companies with slowing growth should market harder, but $120M+ in sponsorship sales taught him the opposite: pushing a product the market has moved away from is a grind with diminishing returns, while reminding warm prospects to buy now compounds.
