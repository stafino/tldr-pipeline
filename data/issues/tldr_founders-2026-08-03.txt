Headlines & Trends

Sequoia Leads $1 Billion Funding Round in Nuke Startup Valar (1 minute read)

Sequoia led a $1B round in Valar Atomics, capital the nuclear startup says will move it from demonstrating small reactors to producing them at volume. The check size signals that investors now treat advanced nuclear as a manufacturing problem, not a science problem, and are willing to fund the factory buildout that scaling demands. The bet is that AI data centers and reindustrialization create durable baseload demand, and the winners will be whoever can ship reactors as a repeatable product rather than a one-off megaproject.

Palantir posts blowout Q2 numbers, with U.S. commercial revenue soaring nearly 150% (1 minute read)

Palantir posted Q2 revenue of $1.94B, up 93% from a year ago, with net income tripling to $1.07B and the stock jumping 8% on the print. The engine is no longer just government: U.S. commercial revenue surged 149% to $764M and remaining deal value there more than doubled to $6.24B, prompting Palantir to raise full-year guidance to about $8.15B. The numbers cut against the growing bet that the AI software trade is stalling, and Karp is telling investors the run continues for at least another 18 months.

Sequoia leads $1 billion round for Valar Atomics as the nuclear startup triples its valuation to $6 billion (1 minute read)

Sequoia led a $1B Series B for Valar Atomics at a $6B valuation, triple the $2B the nuclear startup carried just months ago. The round funds the shift from demonstration to factory production of small modular reactors, after Valar powered an Nvidia AI chip from a fission reaction in July. Valar and Nvidia are now planning a 30MW nuclear-powered facility in Utah with near-zero water use, aimed squarely at the grid and water objections stalling conventional AI data centers. None of these reactors run commercially outside China and Russia yet, and the industry's track record is late and over budget.

Index Ventures doubles down on AI with fresh $2bn fundraise (5 minute read)

Index Ventures raised a fresh $2B fund and is pointing the bulk of it at AI, doubling down on the sector even as fears of a bubble mount. The firm is betting that the application layer, not just the model labs, is where durable venture returns will come from over the next cycle. For founders, it signals that late-stage capital is still flowing to AI-native companies, but increasingly toward those that can show real traction rather than a demo and a thesis.

Valar, which is making small modular nuclear reactors to power data centers, raised a $1B Series B led by Sequoia at a $6B post-money valuation (Bloomberg) (1 minute read)

Valar raised a $1B Series B led by Sequoia at a $6B post-money valuation to build small modular nuclear reactors that power data centers. The round signals that the AI buildout is now bottlenecked on electricity, not chips, and capital is moving upstream to whoever can supply dedicated baseload. Small modular reactors are still years from grid-scale deployment, so a $6B valuation is a bet that compute demand outruns the timeline every other power source can meet.

The Startup Turning Home Batteries Into Grid Infrastructure (1 minute read)

Base Power raised $1 billion in a Series D at a $13 billion post-money valuation, betting that networked home batteries can function as grid infrastructure rather than backup gear. US electricity demand is climbing fast enough that investors are now funding battery startups as a way to add capacity the grid can't build quickly enough on its own. The pitch is that thousands of distributed home systems, coordinated together, become a virtual power plant utilities can lean on during peak load.

A two-year-old battery startup just hit $13bn by turning American homes into grid capacity. (1 minute read)

Base Power, a battery startup that didn't exist three years ago, raised $1bn in a Series D at a $13bn valuation, pushing its total raised past $2.5bn. The bet is that the grid can't add generation fast enough for AI data centers and electrification, so Base pools home batteries into virtual power plants the grid can lean on at peak. The number is steep for a 2023 company with a 500 MWh fleet live in slices of two states, but the same demand squeeze straining the grid has turned fixing it into a venture-scale prize.

Index Ventures raises €1.7 billion, bringing total investing capital to nearly €3 billion (1 minute read)

Index Ventures raised roughly €1.7 billion, pushing its total fresh investing capital to nearly €3 billion across a €347 million seed fund, a €780 million venture fund, and €607 million added to its growth fund. The raise lands as the firm turns 30, having ridden early bets like Wiz, which Google bought for $32 billion, to a portfolio that includes Figma, Revolut, and Datadog. Capital at this scale from a top-tier firm signals that seed and venture dry powder is still flowing, even as the wider market sits on a unicorn backlog with few exits.

Two brothers behind K2 Space grab $500M at $6.8B valuation to challenge SpaceX (1 minute read)

K2 Space raised a $500M Series D at a $6.8B valuation, co-led by Kleiner Perkins and ICONIQ, pushing total funding past $1B just four years after founding. Index Ventures raises $3.5B for AI boom its co-founder called unsustainable (1 minute read)

Dear SaaStr:  I Fired My VP of Sales.  What Now? (1 minute read)

Firing your VP of Sales feels like a crisis, but the fix is usually a plan, not another quick hire. Step in as interim yourself or back a strong Director or AE who is a stretch, because you know the product and customers better than any replacement will on day one. Then audit revenue per lead, close rates, and cycle length to find what was actually broken before you hire again. A bad VP hire can set you back a year, so keep the pipeline moving and take the time to find the right fit.
