Index Ventures raises €1.7 billion, bringing total investing capital to nearly €3 billion (1 minute read)

Index Ventures raised roughly €1.7 billion, pushing its total fresh investing capital to nearly €3 billion across a €347 million seed fund, a €780 million venture fund, and €607 million added to its growth fund. The raise lands as the firm turns 30, having ridden early bets like Wiz, which Google bought for $32 billion, to a portfolio that includes Figma, Revolut, and Datadog. Capital at this scale from a top-tier firm signals that seed and venture dry powder is still flowing, even as the wider market sits on a unicorn backlog with few exits.

Two brothers behind K2 Space grab $500M at $6.8B valuation to challenge SpaceX (1 minute read)

K2 Space raised a $500M Series D at a $6.8B valuation, co-led by Kleiner Perkins and ICONIQ, pushing total funding past $1B just four years after founding. The valuation more than doubled in roughly seven months, up from a $3B Series C in December 2025. K2's contrarian bet is that bigger, higher-power satellites, not the industry's swarm of small cheap ones, will win the AI-compute and defense era, and it has already signed over $1B in contracts with SES, Anduril, and the US Space Force while preparing to build up to 100 large satellites a year.

A look at the US open-weight AI model ecosystem, as VCs question the revenue potential of open-weight startups like Arcee, Reflection AI, and Poolside (Wall Street Journal) (1 minute read)

Open-weight AI startups are shipping capable models, but VCs are now openly questioning where the revenue comes from. Companies like Arcee, Reflection AI, and Poolside can match closed labs on quality, yet giving the weights away undercuts the obvious subscription and API business that funds the closed players. The open question is whether the money lives in services, fine-tuning, and enterprise deployment around the model, because the model itself is turning into a commodity that anyone can run.

Index Ventures raises $3.5B for AI boom its co-founder called unsustainable (1 minute read)

Index Ventures raised $2B in new funds, pushing its investable capital to $3.5B, and much of it is going straight back into the AI market that produced the returns. The timing is stark: the raise follows a roughly $3.8B payout from Google's $32B acquisition of Wiz and nearly $9B in exits over the prior year, yet co-founder Neil Rimer recently said AI wealth will face "some sort of a redistribution," either voluntary or forced. The firm's actions and its founder's warning point in opposite directions, with 41% of all venture dollars now chasing the same sector.

How OpenAI Lost Its AI Crown—and the Fight to Win It Back (1 minute read)

OpenAI still leads on consumer mindshare, but Anthropic quietly won the market that pays: AI coding. While OpenAI poured attention into chatbots and flashy side projects, Anthropic aimed at code generation, the workflow developers now run through Claude and pay for on repeat. The lesson for founders is that the loudest category isn't always the most defensible one, and the revenue tends to accrue to whoever owns the boring, high-frequency workflow rather than the demo everyone shares.

Europe starts enforcing AI Act rules (5 minute read)

The EU has started enforcing the first binding obligations under the AI Act, with new transparency rules landing on companies that build or deploy AI systems in the bloc. Providers now have to disclose when users are interacting with AI, label synthetic content, and document how general-purpose models were trained. The compliance work falls hardest on smaller teams that shipped into Europe without a legal function, and the practical move is to treat disclosure and model documentation as product requirements now rather than scramble when the fines start.

German Robotics Startup Agile Robots Set to Double Revenue This Year (1 minute read)

Agile Robots, the German startup, expects to double revenue this year on a bet that manufacturing expertise, not AI advances alone, will drive the surge and finally produce profits. The wager runs against the prevailing story that model progress is the whole game in robotics, and it puts the hard part back on the factory floor. For founders in physical-world categories, it is a reminder that the durable edge often sits in operations and production, not the model layer everyone is racing to fund.

Wall Street Thinks It Knows How Tech Giants Will Make AI Pay (1 minute read)

Wall Street has landed on a simple test for whether Big Tech's AI spending is worth it: is there a cloud-computing business to sell the capacity into. Investors are warming to the capex only when it feeds a rentable platform like Azure, AWS, or Google Cloud, where AI demand converts into billable usage rather than a research line item. The read for founders is that the market now rewards AI infrastructure with a clear path to recurring revenue, and discounts spending that can't point to who pays for it.

US AI startups pay engineers 4 times more than European ones, and the reason has nothing to do with talent (1 minute read)

US AI startups pay engineers around four times what European ones pay, and the gap tracks capital, not skill. American companies raise far larger rounds and sit on deeper compute budgets, so they can afford to bid salaries European founders simply can't match on the same cap tables. The result is a talent drain that compounds, as the best European engineers follow the money across the Atlantic and the funding gap turns into a hiring gap that widens every cycle.

Scale AI just hired a Google Cloud security chief as CEO: Does that tell you everything about where its money will come from? (1 minute read)

Scale AI named Francis deSouza, formerly Google Cloud's security and threat-intelligence chief, as CEO effective August 10, signaling where the board expects future revenue to come from. The company built its business selling labeled training data to AI labs like OpenAI and Meta, but is now chasing government, defense, and enterprise contracts with clients like BP and the Mayo Clinic, where trust and compliance are the whole sale. Hiring a security operator over another founder tells you Scale is repositioning from an AI-lab data vendor into a compliance-heavy government contractor.

Dear SaaStr:  I Fired My VP of Sales.  What Now? (1 minute read)

Firing your VP of Sales feels like a crisis, but the fix is usually a plan, not another quick hire. Step in as interim yourself or back a strong Director or AE who is a stretch, because you know the product and customers better than any replacement will on day one. Then audit revenue per lead, close rates, and cycle length to find what was actually broken before you hire again. A bad VP hire can set you back a year, so keep the pipeline moving and take the time to find the right fit.
