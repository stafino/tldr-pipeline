Bending Spoons strikes $2.25bn Airtable deal in first acquisition since IPO (5 minute read)

Bending Spoons agreed to acquire Airtable for $2.25bn, its first deal since a July Nasdaq listing at an $18bn valuation. The Milan company runs a buy-rebuild-integrate playbook, having absorbed Evernote, WeTransfer, Vimeo, Eventbrite and AOL onto one shared platform, with roughly 80% of its 700 staff working as engineers and operators who rebuild the products it buys. Unlike the private equity firms it gets compared to, it has never sold a business, so Airtable's no-code workflow tooling becomes a permanent part of the stack rather than a flip.

Index Ventures doubles down on AI with fresh $2bn fundraise (5 minute read)

Index Ventures raised a fresh $2B fund and is pointing the bulk of it at AI, doubling down on the sector even as fears of a bubble mount. The firm is betting that the application layer, not just the model labs, is where durable venture returns will come from over the next cycle. For founders, it signals that late-stage capital is still flowing to AI-native companies, but increasingly toward those that can show real traction rather than a demo and a thesis.

A Record 14 Billion-Dollar Rounds In July Pushed Venture’s Historic Run Higher (1 minute read)

Global venture funding hit $65B in July, up 100% year over year, with a record 14 billion-dollar rounds in a single month. AI took $35B of that, around 53% of the global total, led by Blue Origin's $10B, Safe Superintelligence's reported $5B from Nvidia, and Moonshot AI's $3.5B. The run follows a first half where startups raised $515B, so capital is concentrating in fewer, larger bets across AI, defense, and energy while the long tail keeps getting squeezed out.

A high school dropout’s nuclear startup just landed $1B from Sequoia at a $6B valuation (1 minute read)

Valar Atomics raised $1B in Series B led by Sequoia at a $6B valuation, tripling the mark it carried after its April round. The timing followed hard milestones: its Ward 250 reactor reached self-sustaining criticality in June, then powered an Nvidia Blackwell chip in a live demo. The bet is that nuclear's real bottleneck was never physics but manufacturing, so Valar builds small gas-cooled reactors for factory production and grows its own fuel. As AI electricity demand climbs, capital is moving past chips and data centers into the power systems underneath them.

5 Interesting Learnings from Palantir at $7.7 Billion ARR: 93% Growth (!), 157% NRR, and a Rule of 40 Score of 155% (1 minute read)

Palantir grew revenue 93% year-over-year to $1.935B, its twelfth straight quarter of accelerating growth, and mid-year it raised full-year guidance by roughly half a billion dollars to $8.15B. Net revenue retention hit 157% and its Rule of 40 score reached 155%, all off a base of just 1,049 total customers. The lesson for founders is that deceleration at scale is a default, not a law. It holds when your product is a mature category replacement, but it breaks when you sit on top of a platform shift that just got budget.

Anthropic signs $10 billion deal with AI cloud startup Volta (1 minute read)

Anthropic signed a reported $10B deal with Volta, a cloud startup founded earlier this year, to supply Claude with compute over six years. The capacity comes from a new 133-megawatt Norway data center, built with crypto miner Bitdeer and running Nvidia's Vera Rubin systems. It follows recent compute deals with SpaceX and Amazon, and the pattern is clear: Anthropic is locking up capacity anywhere it can find it, including from a barely-year-old startup and a crypto-mining partner, because the constraint on frontier labs right now is power and chips, not customers.

Microsoft Earnings, Microsoft vs. Meta, The Efficiency Payoff (1 minute read)

Microsoft's latest earnings landed well not because of raw AI spend but because of the opposite: a clear strategy, falling costs, and AI showing up in products customers actually pay for. The contrast with Meta is the real story, since Microsoft is turning capex into tangible application revenue while its rival is still funding a bet that hasn't converted. The uncomfortable read for operators is that the efficiency payoff, not the model race, is starting to separate the companies that win on AI from the ones just spending on it.

Bending Spoons to buy Airtable for $1.28B (1 minute read)

Bending Spoons agreed to buy Airtable for $1.28B in cash, its first acquisition since going public at an $18B valuation last month. Airtable raised more than $1.4B and peaked at over $11B in 2021, but sold at roughly a fifth of that after secondary shares traded near $4B earlier this year. Bending Spoons runs a fixed playbook on the brands it buys, from Evernote to Vimeo: acquire at a discount to the private mark, trim staff, streamline the product, and make it run profitably.

Deel acquires deepfake-detection startup Clarity as fake hire problem grows (1 minute read)

Deel acquired deepfake-detection startup Clarity for up to $50M, its 15th acquisition, to fold continuous identity verification into hiring, payroll, and IT access. The bet is that fake hires are becoming a structural problem, with Gartner predicting one in four candidate profiles globally will be fraudulent by 2028 as cheap AI clears video interviews. Most companies verify identity once at recruitment, then hand off to separate IT systems, and Deel's argument is that the handoff is the weak point a fraudulent hire only needs to clear once.

HappyRobot lands $150M Series C to scale agentic AI for enterprise operations (1 minute read)

HappyRobot raised $150M in Series C at a $1.2B valuation, led by Prysm Capital and co-led by Eurazeo, after growing 5x since its Series B late last year. The company deploys AI agents that execute and reason inside existing enterprise systems across voice, email, documents, and the web, with one customer automating 28,000 hours of work a month and support agents resolving more than 70% of cases autonomously. Having proven the platform in logistics with DHL, Kuehne + Nagel, and Uber, it is now pushing into insurance, energy, telecom, and airlines.

EU’s €5bn tech superfund officially open for business (5 minute read)

The European Commission's €5bn Scaleup Europe Fund cleared its legal proceedings and is ready to invest, with first deals expected within weeks. Run by Swedish firm EQT, it will typically lead or co-lead rounds of €100m to €500m from a pipeline of more than 100 startups, with Mistral's targeted €3bn raise reportedly among the early targets. The bet is that Europe's fastest-growing companies stop crossing the Atlantic for late-stage capital, though EQT is still fundraising the rest of the €5bn from LPs like Novo Holdings, Allianz, and Wallenberg Investments.

European robotics startups raised more in six months than past two years combined (5 minute read)

European robotics startups raised more equity in the first half of 2026 than in the previous two years combined, led by Neura Robotics's €1.2bn round, the continent's biggest robotics raise on record. The surge is a handful of mega-deals rather than a broad wave. Deal count actually fell from 48 in Q1 to 26 in Q2, and most of the roughly 97 startups that raised stayed under €35m.

AI unicorn Olix valued at $3.3bn after $312m Series B raise (1 minute read)

Two-year-old London chip startup Olix raised $312m in Series B at a $3.3bn valuation, backed by Arm, Hudson River Trading, Reed Hastings, and the UK government's Sovereign AI fund. Rather than force one chip to do everything, Olix builds specialized silicon connected by an optical network, shipping complete racks tuned for frontier inference. The bet is that the industry's supply-chain crunch gets solved at the system level, not the single-chip level, and investors are now funding that thesis at unicorn scale barely a year after the company was founded.

Why smarter AI models could drive up compute prices 10x (1 minute read)

The consensus assumption is that compute keeps getting cheaper, but Dwarkesh Patel argues the opposite could hold: as models get smarter, each unit of compute produces more valuable work, so demand outruns falling hardware costs and inference prices could climb up to 10x. The teams betting their margins on cheap tokens are exposed, because the same capability gains that make AI worth deploying are what bid up the compute underneath it. If your unit economics only work at today's prices, model the version where they invert.

GTM in 2026: What's Actually Changed (1 minute read)

Sales cycles compressed from 25 weeks to 19 in a single year, while sub-one-year contracts jumped from 4% of deals to 13% and three-year deals slipped to 23%. Both numbers trace to the same buyer fear: move fast, commit to little. The ICONIQ State of Go-to-Market 2026 report, built on 150+ B2B GTM executives, shows the 2021 playbook of adding reps and buying leads is done. With more deals moving at once and every call earning the next one, the seller who remembers exactly what call one asked for is the one who wins call two.

Is the future of data centers portable? Runware builds a pod to find out (1 minute read)

Runware launched the Sonic Inference Pod, a single transportable modular data center that runs inference at higher quality and lower cost than serverless platforms and GPU clouds. Capacity scales by adding pods instead of expanding a fixed facility, deploys anywhere there is power, and uses closed-loop cooling built in days rather than the months or years traditional sites take. Ten pods are live across the US, Europe, and Asia-Pacific, serving customers including Higgsfield AI and Wix, with 160 sites ready to host more.
