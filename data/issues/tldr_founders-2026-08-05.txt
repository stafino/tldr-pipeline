Polymarket in talks to raise $1B at $20B valuation four months after $15B valuation round (1 minute read)

Polymarket is in early talks to raise around $1B at a valuation above $20B, four months after closing a $15B round backed by ICE. Annualized revenue has climbed past $1B since that raise, driven by the May launch of its regulated US exchange, where daily volume now tops $100M. The number to watch is Kalshi, which raised at $22B in May and is reportedly targeting $40B on roughly $1.5B in annualized revenue. Prediction markets have consolidated into a two-horse race, and both horses are repricing every few months.

Oxide Computer raises $445M (SEC Form D) (1 minute read)

Oxide Computer raised $445M according to a new SEC Form D filing, one of the largest hardware rounds in recent memory. The company sells fully integrated server racks, hardware, firmware, and software built as one system, pitched at enterprises that want cloud-style operations on their own premises instead of renting from hyperscalers. A raise this size signals that on-prem is drawing real capital again, driven by companies weighing the cost of AI compute and the pull of owning their own infrastructure rather than leasing it indefinitely.

The AI frenzy has divided the VC market, as the gap between top and bottom performers has more than doubled for 2024 funds compared to funds from 2017 to 2021 (Bloomberg) (1 minute read)

The AI frenzy has split the venture market in two. The spread between top and bottom performers has more than doubled for 2024 funds versus the 2017 to 2021 vintages, as capital concentrates in a handful of AI winners and leaves the rest behind. Fund vintage now matters less than whether a firm got into the right AI names, which widens the gap between the funds LPs chase and the ones they quietly write off.

London AI cloud startup Volta exits stealth at €2.07 billion valuation, lands €8.6 billion compute deal reportedly with Anthropic (1 minute read)

Volta emerged from stealth at a €2.07 billion valuation on just €259.8 million ($300 million) raised, backed by a16z, Altimeter, NVIDIA, and Azora. The pull was demand, not the round: the company simultaneously landed a €8.6 billion ($10 billion) compute deal reportedly with Anthropic to build a European AI factory alongside Bitdeer. Compute is being financed like infrastructure now, with off-balance-sheet capital funding the data centers that frontier labs can't build alone, and the AI cloud layer between NVIDIA and the labs is where the next tier of European giants is forming.

Scaleup Europe Fund makes first investment, co-leading Iceye's €1BN round (1 minute read)

The EU's €5bn Scaleup Europe Fund, managed by EQT, made its first investment, co-leading Finnish satellite startup Iceye's €1bn Series F at a roughly €10bn valuation alongside General Atlantic. The fund exists to keep European challengers in strategic sectors like AI, quantum, space, and biotech growing at home instead of decamping to the US for capital. Iceye is a fitting first bet, having gone from an Erasmus meeting and Horizon 2020 grants to a €10bn radar-imaging company, which is exactly the homegrown-to-global arc Brussels wants the fund to underwrite.

EQT-Run Scaleup Europe Fund Invests in Satellite Startup Iceye (1 minute read)

Europe's new €5 billion ($5.8 billion) technology fund, run by EQT, has disclosed that its first check goes to Iceye, the Finnish maker of synthetic-aperture radar satellites. The Scaleup Europe Fund is part of the bloc's push to keep strategic-technology companies growing on the continent instead of raising later rounds in the US. The signal for founders is where European capital is now concentrating: defense, space, and dual-use hardware are being treated as sovereignty bets, and the money is following the companies that anchor supply chains the region does not want to import.

Moove Hits $2.1 Billion Valuation in Mubadala-Led Fundraising (1 minute read)

Moove raised $250M at a $2.1B valuation in a round led by Abu Dhabi sovereign fund Mubadala. The Nigerian-founded startup began by financing vehicles for gig drivers and is now using the capital to add self-driving fleet services and push into new markets. Sovereign wealth money flowing into emerging-market fintech signals that the fleet-financing layer beneath ride-hailing and autonomy is becoming its own investable category, not just a feature of the platforms that sit on top of it.

SpaceX, in First Earnings After IPO, Reports Soaring AI Spending (1 minute read)

SpaceX reported its first earnings since going public, and capital expenditures jumped nearly seven times from a year ago as AI spending surged alongside rising revenue. The company is following the same playbook as the hyperscalers, pouring cash into compute and infrastructure ahead of the returns. For founders, it signals that the capex arms race now reaches beyond the cloud giants into every capital-intensive company betting its future on AI.
