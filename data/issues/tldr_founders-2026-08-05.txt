Oxide Computer raises $445M (SEC Form D) (1 minute read)

Oxide Computer raised $445M according to a new SEC Form D filing, one of the largest hardware rounds in recent memory. The company sells fully integrated server racks, hardware, firmware, and software built as one system, pitched at enterprises that want cloud-style operations on their own premises instead of renting from hyperscalers. A raise this size signals that on-prem is drawing real capital again, driven by companies weighing the cost of AI compute and the pull of owning their own infrastructure rather than leasing it indefinitely.

London AI cloud startup Volta exits stealth at €2.07 billion valuation, lands €8.6 billion compute deal reportedly with Anthropic (1 minute read)

Volta emerged from stealth at a €2.07 billion valuation on just €259.8 million ($300 million) raised, backed by a16z, Altimeter, NVIDIA, and Azora. The pull was demand, not the round: the company simultaneously landed a €8.6 billion ($10 billion) compute deal reportedly with Anthropic to build a European AI factory alongside Bitdeer. Compute is being financed like infrastructure now, with off-balance-sheet capital funding the data centers that frontier labs can't build alone, and the AI cloud layer between NVIDIA and the labs is where the next tier of European giants is forming.

Yann LeCun’s second attempt at a VC fund lands with $100M and Gemini’s co-lead as a partner (1 minute read)

Yann LeCun and former Gemini co-technical lead Oriol Vinyals joined 224 Ventures, a new AI fund launching with more than $100M under management. The firm writes $1M to $5M checks into early-stage AI startups valued under $100M, targeting research talent building capital-efficient models, robotics, and infrastructure, with the University of Illinois as anchor investor. The signal for founders is that frontier researchers are now packaging their reputations into formal investing vehicles, and Vinyals is doing it the same day he left DeepMind to co-found a science-automation startup.

The startup idea that convinced a UW computer science legend to leave Google after 27 years (1 minute read)

Jeff Dean is leaving Google after 27 years to start Discovery Loop, a public benefit corporation that automates the full scientific research loop: proposing experiments, running them, evaluating results, and iterating thousands of times. He is taking Sanjay Ghemawat, Oriol Vinyals, and Quoc Le with him, with Google itself as founding investor and compute supplier for the first year. When the people who built modern AI infrastructure bet on a lean team automating discovery, the signal to founders is that the next frontier is applying AI to science, not just to software.

Jeff Dean and three other Google execs are leaving to launch Discovery Loop, which will seek AI-powered breakthroughs in drug discovery, chip design, and more (Steven Levy/Wired) (1 minute read)

Jeff Dean and three other Google executives are leaving to start Discovery Loop, a company aiming AI at breakthroughs in drug discovery, chip design, and other hard scientific problems. Moove raises $250M to become the backbone of the robotaxi industry (1 minute read)

Moove raised $250M at a $2.1B valuation, led by Mubadala, to expand from vehicle financing and human-driven ride-hailing into owning and operating robotaxi fleets. The company already runs a 42,000-vehicle fleet across 14 countries and serves as Waymo's fleet operator in Phoenix, Miami, and Las Vegas, with London next. Its bet is that no one in the AV stack, not the developers, manufacturers, or marketplaces, wants to own and service the metal, so Moove is positioning to own, operate, and maintain hundreds of thousands of vehicles for them.

SpaceX, in First Earnings After IPO, Reports Soaring AI Spending (1 minute read)

SpaceX reported its first earnings since going public, and capital expenditures jumped nearly seven times from a year ago as AI spending surged alongside rising revenue. The company is following the same playbook as the hyperscalers, pouring cash into compute and infrastructure ahead of the returns. For founders, it signals that the capex arms race now reaches beyond the cloud giants into every capital-intensive company betting its future on AI.
