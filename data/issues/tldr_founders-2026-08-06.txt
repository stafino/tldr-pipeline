Airtable Just Sold for $2.25B at 2.7x ARR. It Raised $1.4B and Was Once Worth $11.7B. This Was … Market (1 minute read)

Airtable sold to Bending Spoons for roughly $2.25B, or 2.7x ARR, after raising $1.4B and hitting an $11.7B valuation in 2021. The refounding worked: it went AI-native under a founder-CEO who returned to writing code, cut to cash-flow positive throwing off over $100M a year, and holds 80% of the Fortune 100 on about $480M ARR. But it grew 20%, not 60%, and at 20% growth you get priced like a cash-flow asset, not a compounder. Executing the AI pivot well stabilized the business; it did not buy back the multiple.

Google Earnings, The Frontier Case, Amazon Earnings (1 minute read)

Google's latest earnings read as confirmation of its Anthropic hedge, with the search giant leaning on a partner it also competes with to keep pace at the frontier. Andy Jassy made the sharper case for the spending, arguing that Amazon's and Google's capex is justifiable because frontier demand is real and the cost of underbuilding compute is worse than the cost of overbuilding it. For founders, the takeaway is that the hyperscalers now treat massive AI infrastructure bets as defensive necessities rather than optional wagers, and that framing sets the terms everyone downstream builds on.

PitchBook: AI voice startups raised $7B in Q1 '26, vs. $1B in Q1 '25, as OpenAI and Google bet on voice as the main interface for a new generation of AI agents (Cristina Criddle/Financial Times) (1 minute read)

AI voice startups raised $7B in Q1 '26, up from $1B a year earlier, as OpenAI and Google position voice as the primary interface for the next wave of AI agents. The seven-fold jump reflects a bet that spoken interaction, not text or clicks, becomes how most people direct agents day to day. For founders, the moat is shifting from the model to the interaction layer, and the incumbents are moving to own it before the products built on them can.

SpaceX Reports Q2 Earnings (1 minute read)

SpaceX's first earnings as a public company confirm it's no longer a rocket company, posting $7.8B in revenue up 92% year-over-year across space, Starlink, and AI. Starlink now carries the business, contributing 55% of revenue and $1.7B in operating income while the launch segment lost $542M to Starship R&D and the AI unit burned $15.8B in capex for a $1.3B loss. The stock still fell 7% after hours as investors weighed heavy spending and a lockup expiry, a reminder that a diversified moat and a $100B cash pile don't buy patience for losses.

AI makes weather prediction better. Can WindBorne make it lucrative? (1 minute read)

WindBorne Systems raised a $37M Series B co-led by Khosla Ventures and Galvanize, valuing the weather-data startup at $250M. AI forecasting models cracked open a business that was closed to private companies for years, since simulating the atmosphere used to require supercomputers. WindBorne's moat is its proprietary data: about 600 long-endurance balloons collecting readings from places like the eye of a typhoon, now expanding into ocean buoys. Government agencies buy the data today, but the real prize is commercial, starting with investment funds that trade weather signals into commodity bets.

“Our AI Agent Rewrote Our App Without Telling Us” The Agents #12 is Here! (1 minute read)

The distinction that matters with AI agents is task versus decision: when an agent can only do a task, you check the task, but when it can decide, you need an opinion about every decision it makes. Jason Lemkin's team runs three humans and 20+ agents, and their daily agent time went from 30 minutes combined a year ago to eight hours each, because a first-party agent generates an infinite backlog and the constraint is now your attention, not engineering capacity.
