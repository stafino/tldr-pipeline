Defense tech company Hadrian raised a $1.37B Series D at an $8B valuation to expand its factories and Opus software platform, after a $260M Series C in 2025 (Colin Demarest/Axios) (1 minute read)

Hadrian raised a $1.37B Series D at an $8B valuation to expand its automated factories and Opus software platform, more than 5x the valuation implied by its $260M Series C in 2025. The round signals that defense manufacturing has become one of venture's hottest bets, as investors chase startups rebuilding the industrial base for drones, missiles, and satellites. The money is going into physical capacity and the software that runs it, a bet that the next defense primes will win on factory throughput rather than legacy contracts.

A look at Sequoia's revamped strategy under new stewards Alfred Lin and Pat Grady, including bold AI bets; sources: Sequoia recently closed $10B in new funding (Bloomberg) (1 minute read)

Sequoia closed $10B in fresh funding and handed the reins to Alfred Lin and Pat Grady, who are steering the firm toward more concentrated AI bets. The revamp leans into backing a smaller set of frontier and application-layer companies rather than spreading capital across the field. For founders, it signals that the most established venture brand is now competing hardest for the same AI deals everyone else wants, and it has $10B to press the point.

Startup Raises $700 Million to Replace Data-Center Wires With Light (1 minute read)

Lumilens raised $700M at a $5.5B valuation to replace the copper wiring between AI servers with optical links. As models scale, the bottleneck has shifted from raw chip speed to how fast data moves between them, and light carries far more bandwidth per watt than the copper interconnects most data centers still run on. The size of the round signals that investors now see networking, not just GPUs, as the next hard constraint on AI infrastructure.

How Mechanize went from a $9M seed to $1.5B Google acquisition talks in just 100 days (1 minute read)

Mechanize raised a $9.1M seed at a $500M valuation on April 24, and 103 days later Google was in talks to pay more than $1.5B for its technology and engineering team. This is Google's third reverse acqui-hire in two years, after Character AI and Windsurf, and the pattern is now clear: license the tech, hire the people, skip the company. What draws the interest is focus. While rivals like Prime Intellect build sprawling open-source hubs of RL environments, Mechanize bet on a narrow set of high-quality coding evaluations for frontier labs.

WindBorne’s AI beats the government’s weather forecasts. Making money from them is the harder problem (1 minute read)

WindBorne raised a $37M Series B co-led by Khosla Ventures and Galvanize at a $250M valuation, backing a weather AI that already tops public benchmarks and beats the physics models governments run. The moat is the data, not the model, since its 600 balloons gather readings satellites and weather stations miss and anyone can train on public datasets. Turning that edge into a business is the harder bet, because selling sensing data to the private sector has broken startups before, with earth-observation networks retreating to government buyers after a decade of trying.

European AI funding hit $23B in H1 2026 but the US raised 14 times more (1 minute read)

European AI startups raised $23B in the first half of 2026, but US companies pulled in roughly 14 times more over the same period. The gap is widening at the frontier, where capital concentration compounds: the biggest rounds, the most compute, and the strongest talent keep flowing to a handful of US labs. For European founders, the practical read is that the money for capital-intensive foundation models sits across the Atlantic, and the defensible plays at home are the application and workflow layers where proximity to customers still matters more than raw funding.

Omilia raises $67M to scale its customer support platform (1 minute read)

Omilia raised a $67M Series B led by Expedition Growth Capital, betting that the customer-support winners will be the ones with real ROI rather than the flashiest generative-AI story. The Athens-based company has grown ARR 10x to $60M since 2020 by routing simple queries like account-balance checks to cheap tools and reserving LLMs for the cases that actually need them, unlike Sierra, Decagon, and Parloa that lead with generative AI. The bet for founders is that unit economics, not model horsepower, decides who survives once the contact-center hype cools.
