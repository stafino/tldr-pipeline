Airtable Just Sold for $2.25B at 2.7x ARR. It Raised $1.4B and Was Once Worth $11.7B. This Was … Market (1 minute read)

Airtable sold to Bending Spoons for roughly $2.25B, or 2.7x ARR, after raising $1.4B and hitting an $11.7B valuation in 2021. The refounding worked: it went AI-native under a founder-CEO who returned to writing code, cut to cash-flow positive throwing off over $100M a year, and holds 80% of the Fortune 100 on about $480M ARR. But it grew 20%, not 60%, and at 20% growth you get priced like a cash-flow asset, not a compounder. Executing the AI pivot well stabilized the business; it did not buy back the multiple.

Sources: Stripe recently entered exclusive talks to buy OpenRouter in a cash-and-stock deal that would value the startup for close to $10B (The Information) (1 minute read)

Stripe entered exclusive talks to acquire OpenRouter in a cash-and-stock deal valuing the model-routing startup at close to $10B. The buy would fold the leading multi-model API layer, which lets developers route requests across hundreds of models through one endpoint, directly into Stripe's payments and infrastructure stack. It also marks Stripe pushing further beyond payments into the AI developer tooling that its own customers increasingly depend on, and signals that the routing layer between apps and frontier labs is becoming strategic territory worth billions.

Google Earnings, The Frontier Case, Amazon Earnings (1 minute read)

Google's latest earnings read as confirmation of its Anthropic hedge, with the search giant leaning on a partner it also competes with to keep pace at the frontier. Andy Jassy made the sharper case for the spending, arguing that Amazon's and Google's capex is justifiable because frontier demand is real and the cost of underbuilding compute is worse than the cost of overbuilding it. For founders, the takeaway is that the hyperscalers now treat massive AI infrastructure bets as defensive necessities rather than optional wagers, and that framing sets the terms everyone downstream builds on.

DeepSeek Resumes $8 Billion Round With Monolith in the Running (1 minute read)

DeepSeek has restarted its second funding round, targeting close to $8 billion, with Monolith Management among the investors now in talks to contribute. The raise signals that the Chinese lab intends to keep spending against OpenAI and Anthropic on training compute, not slow down after its earlier open-weight releases pulled market attention. For founders building on top of frontier models, another well-funded lab at the frontier means more capable open options and continued downward pressure on inference pricing.

Sources: Nscale told potential backers it generated $100M+ in Q2 revenue, up from $37M in Q1, and has $51B in contracts, ahead of a possible September US IPO (Bailey Lipschultz/Bloomberg) (1 minute read)

Nscale told potential backers it pulled in more than $100M in Q2 revenue, up from $37M in Q1, and is sitting on $51B in signed contracts ahead of a possible September US IPO. The near-tripling in a single quarter shows how fast capital is rotating into the AI cloud layer as demand for GPU capacity outruns supply. The $51B backlog is the real signal, locking in years of revenue before the company has even priced its shares.

Defense Startup Hadrian Valued at $7.87 Billion in New Round (1 minute read)

Hadrian raised at a $7.87B valuation, the latest sign that defense manufacturing is now venture's favorite hard-tech bet. The startup builds automated factories that turn out precision parts for the Pentagon, Lockheed Martin, and other primes, and the new money goes toward hiring to expand that capacity. The signal for founders is that the defense industrial base, long a closed world of legacy contractors, is now open to startups that can actually produce at scale.

Airtable Acquisition Is ‘Kick in the Gut’ for Software Unicorns and Their Backers (1 minute read)

Bending Spoons is buying Airtable for a fraction of the roughly $11B valuation it carried at its 2021 peak, and the discount is the story. The signal for founders and their backers is that a decade of pandemic-era markups is being repriced in public, with a marquee SaaS name changing hands well below what late-stage investors paid in. The buyers winning right now are the disciplined operators like Bending Spoons that acquire good products cheaply and run them lean, not the growth funds still holding paper marks they can't exit.

Nik Storonsky in talks over new share deal for $500bn Revolut valuation (5 minute read)

Revolut CEO Nik Storonsky is in talks for a new share deal that would hand him a stock windfall if the fintech reaches roughly a $500bn valuation, according to the Financial Times. That target sits far above the company's own ambitions, with Revolut aiming for $200bn in a possible 2028 IPO and last valued at $115bn in a recent employee share sale. Storonsky already holds about 29% and has stacked earlier deals tied to $150bn and $200bn milestones, so founder ownership keeps climbing as the goalposts move higher.

UAE Fund Weighs $6.3 Billion for Japan AI Data Center (1 minute read)

Sovereign AI capital is flowing outward from the Gulf, and Japan is the latest destination. A UAE fund is weighing as much as ¥1 trillion ($6.3 billion) to build an AI data center in Japan, one of the largest foreign infrastructure bets the country has seen. The money is chasing compute capacity and geographic diversification at once, planting Gulf capital next to a stable, power-rich market outside the US. For founders, it signals that the buildout race is going global, and the location of your compute is becoming a sovereign question, not just a vendor one.

“Our AI Agent Rewrote Our App Without Telling Us” The Agents #12 is Here! (1 minute read)

The distinction that matters with AI agents is task versus decision: when an agent can only do a task, you check the task, but when it can decide, you need an opinion about every decision it makes. Jason Lemkin's team runs three humans and 20+ agents, and their daily agent time went from 30 minutes combined a year ago to eight hours each, because a first-party agent generates an infinite backlog and the constraint is now your attention, not engineering capacity.
