Defense tech Hadrian raises $1.37B at $8B valuation (1 minute read)

Hadrian raised $1.37B at a $7.87B valuation, led by WCM, Washington Harbour, Valor, 137 Ventures, and Baillie Gifford, with Founders Fund, a16z, and Lux also in. The company isn't building new AI weapons. It runs automated factories that mass-produce parts for the submarines, aircraft, and vehicles the military already fields, opening its fourth facility in Alabama in March under a $2.4B public/private deal. The defense capital chasing frontier autonomy is now also funding the unglamorous supply chain underneath it.

Startup Raises $700 Million to Replace Data-Center Wires With Light (1 minute read)

Lumilens raised $700M at a $5.5B valuation to replace the copper wiring between AI servers with optical links. As models scale, the bottleneck has shifted from raw chip speed to how fast data moves between them, and light carries far more bandwidth per watt than the copper interconnects most data centers still run on. The size of the round signals that investors now see networking, not just GPUs, as the next hard constraint on AI infrastructure.

AI defence tech startup Hadrian lands $1.4B at $7.9B valuation to modernise US manufacturing (1 minute read)

Hadrian raised $1.37B at a $7.87B valuation, nearly five times where investors priced the defence-manufacturing startup in January. The money isn't chasing new weapons platforms but the production infrastructure beneath them, as Washington pursues a $1.5T defence plan and an industrial base that has shrunk for decades. Hadrian builds AI-run factories for the precision parts that go into rockets, submarines, and fighter jets, and its Opus software already runs in the US Army and Navy. The signal for founders is that capital is flowing to whoever can rebuild American manufacturing capacity, not just design the end product.

5 Interesting Learnings from Shopify at $14B in Revenue: 34% Growth, 18% Free Cash Flow Margins, and AI Orders Up 3x (1 minute read)

Shopify reported $3.58B in Q2 revenue, up 34%, with 18% free cash flow margins, and the stock jumped 18% on the print because AI is accelerating the business rather than eroding it. Traffic and orders from AI channels both tripled year-over-year, and those channels bring in net-new buyers at twice the rate of Google, social, and direct, generating demand instead of re-routing it. The reason is structured data: Shopify Catalog holds over 1 billion machine-readable products, and the companies that win agentic buying will be the ones that own the data agents have to query, not the ones with the best AI.

Scaleup Europe Fund makes first investment, co-leading Iceye's €1BN round (1 minute read)

The EU's €5bn Scaleup Europe Fund, managed by EQT, made its first investment, co-leading Finnish satellite startup Iceye's €1bn Series F at a roughly €10bn valuation alongside General Atlantic. The fund exists to keep European challengers in strategic sectors like AI, quantum, space, and biotech growing at home instead of decamping to the US for capital. Iceye is a fitting first bet, having gone from an Erasmus meeting and Horizon 2020 grants to a €10bn radar-imaging company, which is exactly the homegrown-to-global arc Brussels wants the fund to underwrite.

How Mechanize went from a $9M seed to $1.5B Google acquisition talks in just 100 days (1 minute read)

Mechanize raised a $9.1M seed at a $500M valuation on April 24, and 103 days later Google was in talks to pay more than $1.5B for its technology and engineering team. This is Google's third reverse acqui-hire in two years, after Character AI and Windsurf, and the pattern is now clear: license the tech, hire the people, skip the company. What draws the interest is focus. While rivals like Prime Intellect build sprawling open-source hubs of RL environments, Mechanize bet on a narrow set of high-quality coding evaluations for frontier labs.

The Return Of The Repeat Founder: Inside YC’s Growing Class Of Second-Timers (1 minute read)

Repeat founders are showing up in Y Combinator's cohorts in growing numbers, with a YC dataset of 454 second-timers spanning 2005 through 2026 and a peak of 65 in 2025. The pattern is mostly a two-chapter journey, as 94% went through exactly twice and the typical founder returned about five years later, though nearly 30% came back within two years. The rise is less about higher return rates than a compounding alumni base, since every batch enlarges the pool of experienced founders eligible to run it again.

AI data centre startup Firmus just raised another $2.85 billion at a whopping $15B valuation (1 minute read)

Firmus, an Australian-born AI data centre startup, raised another US$2bn at a US$10.5bn valuation, bringing its total to more than US$3bn in 12 months from Nvidia, Coatue, Blackstone, and Jane Street. The company was worth just US$1.85bn last September, so its valuation has roughly sextupled in under a year on the strength of a US$73bn national buildout, Project Southgate. The capital is chasing physical AI infrastructure, where compute demand is pulling private equity and quant money into energy-hungry data centres faster than local communities can absorb them.

There are two completely different roles called ‘FDE’ (3 minute read)

Forward deployed engineer" has become a hiring buzzword, but the title actually covers two different jobs: the product builder who discovers and ships custom software for each customer, and the platform operator who deploys, configures, and drives adoption of the core product. Most startups copying Palantir want the second and hire the first. Getting the bucket right lets one FDE cover 5-10 accounts and keeps custom work from eroding the core product.
