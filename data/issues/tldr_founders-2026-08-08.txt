AI took three quarters of UK venture funding this year. Its margin problem is still unsolved. (1 minute read)

UK AI companies raised $12.6B in the first half of 2026, more than four times a year earlier and close to three-quarters of all British venture capital. But the money is flowing into businesses whose unit economics break the software template: ICONIQ puts AI product gross margins around 52%, versus the 75% to 85% software has delivered for two decades, and inference climbs from 20% to 23% of spend as products scale rather than shrinking. Compute is a per-query cost that grows with usage, so a business underwater at 100 customers does not get rescued at 1,000.

The US OCC denies Dutch fintech Bunq's application for a national bank charter, citing the need for more clarity on its business plan to expand in the US (Bloomberg) (1 minute read)

The US OCC denied Dutch fintech Bunq's application for a national bank charter, saying it needs more clarity on how the company plans to expand stateside. Bunq is profitable with millions of European users, but a charter is the gate to lending, holding deposits, and operating nationally without leaning on partner banks. For any foreign fintech eyeing the US, the signal is that regulators want a concrete operating plan before the license, not the growth-first sequencing that works in Europe.

The Simple Math Behind a 3x Venture Fund: Why Your Best Investment Needs to Return the Whole Thing (1 minute read)

Returning a 3x net fund is what LPs actually require, and it's harder than it sounds. Cambridge Associates data puts 3x net DPI in the top decile of all venture funds, and Pitchbook analysis of over 1,100 funds shows only about 10% clear that bar, while the top quartile often returns under 2x. For most concentrated Seed and Series A funds, there's really one path that works: your single best investment has to return the entire fund on its own, which is why top VCs chase ownership and outlier upside instead of a spread of tidy 5x wins.
