Cybersecurity Detection Classification with Reasoning-enabled Language Models (1 minute read)

This paper trains a chain-of-thought reasoning LLM to triage SOC alerts, targeting analyst alert fatigue on real human-labeled Windows endpoint detections. The system hits 82.6% test accuracy and, at the high-confidence threshold that governs automated triage, lifts benign recall by 43.0% and malicious recall by 18.3% over a direct-label classifier. ThreatForest: Multi-Agent Attack Tree Generation with Pluggable TTP Framework Mapping (1 minute read)

ThreatForest is a multi-agent system that turns source-code repositories into structured attack trees, maps each step to TTPs across MITRE ATT&CK, CAPEC, and cloud threat matrices, and synthesizes mitigations. Across seven domains, panel-scored quality reached 0.63-0.68 for threat statements, trees, and mitigations, but only 0.29 for TTP mapping. The authors isolate the sentence-transformer embedding encoder, not the multi-agent pipeline, as the accuracy bottleneck, since a single-call baseline more than doubled mapping defensibility.

Granite: A Modular Methodology for Foundational Verification of Hardware-Software Leakage Contracts (1 minute read)

Granite is a methodology for modular, foundational verification that a pipelined RISC processor's cycle-by-cycle timing depends only on the observables named in an ISA leakage contract, ruling out timing side channels for constant-time code. Its leakage-aware refinement composes submodule proofs into a whole-design guarantee and yields a single Rocq theorem on hardware-and-software confidentiality, removing the ISA contract itself from the trusted computing base.

After the Break-In: What Attackers Do Once They're Already Inside (1 minute read)

The takeaway for defenders: post-breach cleanup must assume backdoors, added accounts, and disabled tooling, not just the original entry point.

Securing Agents Across Perplexity's Client Endpoints with Numbat (5 minute read)

Numbat integrates directly with common agent harnesses to enforce security rules and enable rapid detection and response.

Security of World-Model-Based Embodied AI: A Lifecycle of Threats, Defenses, and Evaluation (1 minute read)

This survey maps security threats across the full lifecycle of world-model-based embodied AI, from data construction and representation learning through state grounding, trajectory evaluation, execution, and long-term memory adaptation. The authors show that familiar attack families like poisoning, backdoors, sensor spoofing, prompt injection, and supply-chain compromise take on distinct meanings when they corrupt learned dynamics or safety costs and propagate into physical action. It also flags a duality: world models can act as runtime safety shields but generate predictive safety illusions when compromised or over-trusted.

Detection Engineering in the Era of Semantic Malware (5 minute read)

Researchers from Ben-Gurion, Tel Aviv University, Harvard, and Toronto formalized "promptware," malware delivered via prompt injection that follows its own kill chain, analyzing 36 incidents from 2023 to early 2026. Attacks evolved from two-stage chatbot tricks to four- and five-stage chains achieving persistence, C2, lateral movement, and RCE against tools like Copilot, Cursor, and Microsoft 365 Copilot. Detection engineers face a new problem: malicious tool calls are identical to legitimate ones, differing only in who directs them.

We have proof automation now (1 minute read)

Adam Langley argues that proof automation has advanced enough to make dependently-typed verification practical, pointing to a formally verified zstd implementation in Lean as evidence. Formal methods have historically been niche because proof effort dwarfs implementation, with seL4 spending roughly 10x as long proving as designing and shipping 20x more proof code than C. Cheaper proof automation lowers that barrier for security-critical components like parsers and codecs, where verified memory safety and correctness eliminate whole classes of exploitable bugs.
