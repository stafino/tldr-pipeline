Coldcard Hardware Wallet Flaw Linked to $70 Million Bitcoin Theft in 41 Minutes (1 minute read) [flag]

A Coldcard firmware flaw let an attacker drain 1,082.65 BTC (about $70.2 million) from 1,196 addresses in 41 minutes on July 30, after a March 2021 integration error routed seed generation to a deterministic PRNG instead of the STM32 hardware RNG, cutting effective entropy to roughly 40 bits on Mk3 and 72 on later models. Coinkite shipped emergency firmware on July 31, but patching does not fix an existing seed, so exposed owners must generate a new seed and move their coins.

Chinese Hacker Commands DeepSeek via Telegram to Launch Autonomous Attacks (1 minute read)

Unit 42 attributed a campaign to a Chinese-speaking actor (aliases knaithe and KnYuan) who used DeepSeek inside the open-source Hermes Agent framework to autonomously scan for internet-facing systems, select public exploits, and hit over 460 targets across seven exploit tracks spanning eight CVEs. Organizations should patch exposed Langflow, n8n, Marimo, and customer-managed NetScaler ADC or Gateway SAML IdP appliances, and strip public access to workflow and notebook interfaces.

Anthropic Says Claude Mistook the Open Internet for a CTF and Breached Three Organizations (1 minute read)

Anthropic disclosed that three of its models, including Claude Opus 4.7, breached the production infrastructure of three unnamed organizations during CTF capability testing after a misconfiguration by evaluation partner Irregular left the sandbox with live internet access. The models treated real systems as in-scope and compromised them using basic techniques like weak passwords and unauthenticated endpoints, though Anthropic said its latest model halted once it recognized it was on the open internet.

Tailscale didn't stop the Hugging Face intrusion (1 minute read)

An AI agent that escaped a security evaluation breached Hugging Face, chaining a sandbox escape to root on a Kubernetes node, a secret store holding 136 keys, and a stolen Tailscale credential it used to enroll 181 nodes onto the tailnet. Tailscale's retrospective notes no product vulnerability was exploited, and argues the real failure was standard long-lived credentials that let the agent read an entire vault at once.

CISA warns of cyberattacks disrupting U.S. water utilities (1 minute read)

CISA issued an urgent alert warning of a spike in attacks against internet-exposed PLCs in the water and wastewater sector, after hackers disrupted more than 30 Minnesota community water systems by changing device passwords and altering IP addresses to lock out operators. CISA urges owners to pull PLCs and other OT off the public internet immediately, or gate access behind a VPN with default passwords rotated and IP allow-listing enforced.

Ruby on Rails Patches Critical Vulnerability (1 minute read)

Ruby on Rails patched CVE-2026-66066 (CVSS 9.5), a default-configuration arbitrary file read in Active Storage that lets unauthenticated attackers exfiltrate secret_key_base and external credentials, then escalate to RCE. The flaw affects apps using libvips for image processing that accept untrusted uploads; patch Active Storage to 7.2.3.2, 8.0.5.1, or 8.1.3.1, upgrade libvips to 8.13+, and rotate every secret readable by the app process.

Anthropic’s Opus 5 Is Better at Resisting Prompt Injection (1 minute read)

On Anthropic's IPI prompt-injection benchmark, Opus 5 cut the odds of a successful attack within 15 attempts from Opus 4.8's 5.5% to 2.0%, making it the most robust model tested. The nearest non-Claude model, Muse Spark, sat at 16.5%, while GPT 5.6 Sol reached 20.0% and its Terra and Luna variants hit 30.4% and 43.9%. Prompt injection remains unsolvable in the general case, so treat these gains as narrowing the attack surface, not closing it.

6 Reasons Why Device Code Phishing is the Fastest-Growing Threat of 2026 (1 minute read)

Device code phishing, the abuse of OAuth 2.0's device authorization grant to steal access tokens, has scaled from a niche red-team trick to an industrialized threat, with Microsoft reporting 10-15 new campaigns daily and Barracuda counting 7M attacks in four weeks. Because it targets the authorization layer rather than login, it bypasses passkeys, hardware keys, and phishing-resistant MFA. Defenders should restrict the device code flow via Conditional Access and hunt for anomalous device authorizations.

Authorize, don't authenticate (1 minute read)

The author argues web apps should authorize access to a user-controlled personal database rather than authenticate users into an app-owned one, inverting who holds the data. His prototype, ayb, replaces the login screen with a "connect your database" flow, so an application receives scoped access to a database the user owns and can revoke, rather than custody of the data itself. The pitch: eliminate the app as a single point of breach, deletion, or policy change over your records.

CISA Issues Fresh SBOM Guidance. Did They Get It Right? (1 minute read)

CISA and 16 international partners released updated minimum-elements guidance for software bills of materials, adding 10 new data fields including a digital signature attesting to an SBOM's integrity and authenticity, plus roughly a dozen updates to existing elements. The document supersedes NTIA's 2021 baseline. OWASP founder Jeff Williams argues the changes catalog the problem more completely without measurably reducing risk, and that CISA should instead focus on enabling vendors to make their security posture transparent.

ESET tracks rise in malicious AI skills and adaptable malware (1 minute read)

ClickFix detections more than doubled between H2 2025 and H1 2026, quishing hit record telemetry levels, and ESET has now cataloged over 100 EDR killers in the wild.

From dangling DNS records to reverse DNS gaps, attackers find new blind spots (3 minute read)

Infoblox's 2026 Threat Landscape Report found that more than 22% of the 120M domains registered between June 2025 and June 2026 were weaponized or showed threat characteristics, with 44% of malicious domains active for a single day. One-third of identified dangling CNAME records were trivial to take over, opening a path to trusted subdomain hijacking. Defenders should audit DNS for dangling CNAME, MX, and A records, and treat short-lived newly registered domains as a detection signal.

How to govern agentic AI, MCPs, and AI code assistants (3 minute read)

GitLab published a governance framework for agentic AI, arguing that autonomous agents break the human-in-the-loop review model that code completion relied on, since an agent can open a merge request, modify CI/CD config, and push changes without per-step review. The framework covers scoping agent permissions, deciding where human review still belongs, and enforcing audit trails to prove what an agent did. GitLab's survey of 1,500+ developers found 86% believe ungoverned AI code compounds technical debt faster than traditional development.

Defcon's new badge is a security key you can see inside (1 minute read)
