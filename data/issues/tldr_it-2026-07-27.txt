Flaw in Adobe Extension With 300M Installs Enabled WhatsApp Data Theft (5 minute read)

Adobe patched a UXSS-class cross-origin flaw (CVE-2026-48294) in its Acrobat Chrome extension, which runs in roughly 329M browsers, after Guardio showed the bug could silently scrape a victim's WhatsApp Web chats, contacts, and account details from a single malicious webpage. The attack, dubbed HermeticReader, needed no malware, stolen credentials, or device access, exploiting missing checks in the extension's internal messaging to activate a dormant Adobe integration engine.

Analysis & Opinions

Digital sovereignty is real in Europe. The UK? Not so much (1 minute read)

The author argues that digital sovereignty has become a concrete procurement and policy force in the EU, where regulation and public-sector buying increasingly favor European-controlled cloud and software, while the UK has no comparable strategy and remains dependent on US hyperscalers. For IT leaders, the split matters for vendor selection and data-residency planning: EU buyers gain leverage to demand local alternatives, while UK organizations keep defaulting to AWS, Microsoft, and Google with little sovereign fallback.

How to make Windows a proper development environment (3 minute read)

The author walks through five steps to turn Windows into a serious development environment, centered on Windows Subsystem for Linux for a low-overhead Linux terminal alongside native Windows tooling. The practical takeaway for IT leaders is that Windows fleets can support Linux-first developer workflows without VMs or a switch to macOS, though the setup requires admin rights and per-machine configuration that argues for standardized provisioning images.

Platform engineering for the agentic enterprise (5 minute read)

The author argues that internal developer platforms must be redesigned around a new consumer: AI agents that provision infrastructure, deploy applications, and run operational workflows alongside human engineers. The implication for platform teams is that agents and humans need to interact through different interfaces but the same governance, policy, and access model, so identity, authorization, and audit become the platform's core responsibility rather than deployment automation alone.

The rise of the ‘internet of agents’ and why it changes everything (2 minute read)

The author argues that enterprise AI agents are shifting from internal workflows to cross-company interactions, negotiating with suppliers, insurers, and partner agents beyond the corporate boundary. The gap is infrastructure: without shared identity, standard messaging, or trust models, agent-to-agent workflows collapse into brittle point-to-point integrations that don't scale. The piece frames the emerging "internet of agents" standards effort as the missing layer enterprises will need before external agent coordination becomes reliable.

AI sovereignty through diversification (3 minute read)

The author argues that concentrating enterprise AI on a handful of American vendors is now a strategic liability, and that CIOs should hedge with model diversification and self-hosted open-weight models. Practical steps include routing through gateways like OpenRouter, LiteLLM, and Portkey, and moving core workflows off closed APIs, a shift a June 2026 VentureBeat survey found two-thirds of enterprises had already started before Fable 5's shutdown. Diversification alone falls short while 90% of model spend flows to three US vendors.

AI agents aren't failing because of context—they're failing because of data (5 minute read)

The author argues that enterprise AI agents fail in production not because of poor context engineering but because of stale or silently incomplete data that standard retrieval pipelines never validate for correctness. A relevance-scored pricing document or a record with a missing field passes every check while being wrong, so dashboards stay green as the model answers confidently. The fix belongs upstream in data engineering: validation layers that test whether retrieved data is still correct, not just present and relevant.
