What every CIO needs to know about platform engineering in the age of AI (2 minute read)

The author argues that AI has moved the enterprise bottleneck from writing code to delivering, governing, and running it, making platform engineering the center of AI strategy rather than a back-office function. Autonomous agents are now a distinct class of platform consumer that needs non-human identities, scoped permissions, MCP gateways, audit logging, and budget controls, none of which legacy IDPs were built to provide. The implication for CIOs is that AI readiness depends on extending the internal developer platform to serve agents and operators, not just human developers.

The rise of the ‘internet of agents’ and why it changes everything (2 minute read)

The author argues that enterprise AI agents are shifting from internal workflows to cross-company interactions, negotiating with suppliers, insurers, and partner agents beyond the corporate boundary. The gap is infrastructure: without shared identity, standard messaging, or trust models, agent-to-agent workflows collapse into brittle point-to-point integrations that don't scale. The piece frames the emerging "internet of agents" standards effort as the missing layer enterprises will need before external agent coordination becomes reliable.

AI sovereignty through diversification (3 minute read)

The author argues that concentrating enterprise AI on a handful of American vendors is now a strategic liability, and that CIOs should hedge with model diversification and self-hosted open-weight models. Practical steps include routing through gateways like OpenRouter, LiteLLM, and Portkey, and moving core workflows off closed APIs, a shift a June 2026 VentureBeat survey found two-thirds of enterprises had already started before Fable 5's shutdown. Diversification alone falls short while 90% of model spend flows to three US vendors.

AI agents aren't failing because of context—they're failing because of data (5 minute read)

The author argues that enterprise AI agents fail in production not because of poor context engineering but because of stale or silently incomplete data that standard retrieval pipelines never validate for correctness. A relevance-scored pricing document or a record with a missing field passes every check while being wrong, so dashboards stay green as the model answers confidently. The fix belongs upstream in data engineering: validation layers that test whether retrieved data is still correct, not just present and relevant.
