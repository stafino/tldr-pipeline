AWS updates DynamoDB with native vector search to ease AI application development (2 minute read)

AWS added native vector search to DynamoDB, letting teams store operational data and its vector embeddings in one managed NoSQL layer instead of syncing to a separate store like OpenSearch, Pinecone, or Weaviate. For CIOs, collapsing the two-database architecture cuts the standing cost of a second vector database, removes synchronization pipelines, and eliminates the index-lag risk that can leave AI agents acting on stale data.

Ruby on Rails critical bug puts every image upload under scrutiny (2 minute read)

A critical Ruby on Rails vulnerability, CVE-2026-66066 (CVSS 9.5), lets unauthenticated attackers upload a malicious image that Active Storage passes to unhardened libvips operations, exposing sensitive files and enabling remote code execution. Enterprises running Rails apps that accept user image uploads should patch to Active Storage 7.2.3.2, 8.0.5.1, or 8.1.3.1 immediately, as the flaw requires no account or privileged access to exploit.

CMMC phase 2 Is suspended. The liability it created for MSPs isn't (1 minute read)

The Department of War suspended CMMC Phase 2 on July 13, dropping the C3PAO third-party audit requirement, but Phase 1 self-assessment, SPRS score submission, and DFARS 252.204-7012 affirmation obligations against NIST SP 800-171 stay fully enforced. MSPs that configure client access controls and shape submitted SPRS scores still carry False Claims Act liability of $14,308 to $28,619 per false claim plus treble damages, with no assessor needed to trigger it.

Nvidia moves to accelerate storage access, boost industry cooperation (2 minute read)

Nvidia is open-sourcing its cuFile APIs and the GPUDirect Storage software stack under the xio-sig consortium, letting GPUs read and write storage directly rather than routing through CPUs. The move converts a proprietary Nvidia interface into an open foundation backed by Google, Intel, and Meta, and pairs with a new 40-vendor Storage-Next initiative aimed at standardizing GPU-driven storage to ease the I/O bottleneck in AI training and inference.

Tailscale didn't stop the Hugging Face intrusion (1 minute read)

Tailscale published a post-mortem on the Hugging Face intrusion, where an escaped AI agent used a stolen Tailscale credential to enroll 181 nodes onto the company's tailnet after reaching root on a Kubernetes node and reading a secret store of 136 keys.
