New Attack Methods Enable Malware to Hijack Passkey-Protected Accounts (1 minute read)

Palo Alto Networks researchers disclosed Pass-ta-key, a set of attack methods that let malware already running on a Windows machine hijack Google-synced Chrome passkeys without biometric prompts, privilege escalation, or user interaction. The most severe variant extracts a master secret from Chrome's memory that decrypts every synced passkey for the account, undercutting the phishing-resistance case enterprises use to justify passwordless rollouts on Google's stack.

Prompt injection isn't the bug, AI agent frameworks are (1 minute read)

The author argues that prompt injection is an unsolvable symptom of how AI agent frameworks are built, not a patchable defect, since agents mix untrusted input and privileged actions in the same context by design. For enterprise buyers, the implication is that no vendor patch or guardrail add-on fully closes the hole, and agent deployments should be scoped with least-privilege access and human approval on consequential actions rather than trusted to sanitize inputs.

Adding an API to networking hardware doesn’t solve management challenges (1 minute read)

The author argues that bolting an API onto traditional networking gear doesn't fix management, because the underlying problem is feature inconsistency across devices, even within a single vendor's own operating systems. He points to NAPALM supporting five vendor OSes, three of them Cisco variants, as evidence. Cloud-inspired networks win because virtual switches are uniform, so automation targets one consistent behavior rather than a patchwork of hardware quirks.

Unlocking the future of shared storage: Filestore on Colossus (2 minute read)

Google Cloud rebuilt Filestore, its managed NFS file service, on Colossus, the same distributed storage backend behind YouTube, Gmail, and Gemini, decoupling capacity from performance so IOPS can be provisioned independently without over-provisioning storage. The upgrade extends to GKE through the Filestore CSI driver and multishares that carve a single instance into 10 GiB shares, letting AI teams support thousands of concurrent containers and agent swarms without raising TCO.

Cloudflare launches Identity-Aware AI Gateway to track who is using AI (1 minute read)

Cloudflare launched Identity-Aware AI Gateway, which attaches a verified identity to every AI request leaving a corporate network by wiring AI Gateway into its Cloudflare Access zero-trust product. IT teams can now attribute each prompt to a specific employee or bot, set per-user spending limits, and strip names, passwords, and confidential files from requests before they reach outside model providers.

Red Hat launches new open source project to drive AI governance (1 minute read)

Red Hat launched asago (AI Safety And Governance Orchestration), an open source project that translates corporate and regulatory AI governance policies into operational controls, with early backing from Microsoft, IBM Research, the Alan Turing Institute, and Brave Software. The framework maps requirements to standards like the NIST AI RMF and OWASP LLM Top 10 via the IBM AI Risk Atlas, targeting the policy-to-configuration bottleneck that slows enterprise AI deployments under regimes such as the EU AI Act.

Snowflake hacker pleads guilty, faces up to 32 years in prison (1 minute read)

Connor Moucka, the Canadian behind the 2024 Snowflake breach spree that compromised more than 165 customer environments including AT&T, Ticketmaster, and Santander, pleaded guilty and faces up to 32 years in prison. The attacks used stolen credentials to exfiltrate billions of records on over 100 million people, saddling victim companies with more than $9.5M in losses and underscoring the enterprise cost of unmanaged credential and MFA gaps.
