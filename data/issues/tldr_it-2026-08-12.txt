News & Trends

Nvidia’s $500B AI investment pool could impact enterprise chip pricing, availability (3 minute read)

Nvidia and six finance partners including Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, and Goldman Sachs are creating a $500B-plus capital pool to let its customers, from frontier AI labs to enterprises, buy chips on credit at favorable rates. Analysts expect little of the money to reach enterprise buyers directly, and most warn the infusion will push GPU prices higher and deepen the data-center chip shortage rather than ease procurement.

CISA: Microsoft SharePoint flaw now exploited in ransomware attacks (1 minute read)

CISA added Microsoft SharePoint remote code execution flaw CVE-2026-45659 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog under a ransomware-abused tag, confirming gangs are now exploiting the deserialization bug against unpatched Enterprise Server 2016, 2019, and Subscription Edition servers. Shadowserver counts over 200 of roughly 8,500 exposed SharePoint servers still unpatched, and CISA gave federal agencies three days to apply Microsoft's fixes and enable AMSI and Defender detections.

OpenAI Pauses Astra Over Cybersecurity Risks (5 minute read)

OpenAI paused its Astra model release after internal evaluations flagged that its offensive cybersecurity capabilities crossed the risk threshold in the company's preparedness framework. The hold signals that frontier labs are now gating shipments on security-misuse potential, which gives enterprise buyers a clearer read on vendor guardrails but adds uncertainty to product roadmaps and integration timelines built around expected model availability.

Researchers Disclose AI-Assisted SharePoint Exploit Chain Reaching Unauthenticated RCE (1 minute read)

Rapid7 chained two SharePoint flaws, a JWT-validation bypass (CVE-2026-55040, CVSS 9.1) and a Business Connectivity Services RCE (CVE-2026-63520, CVSS 8.1), into unauthenticated code execution against on-premises SharePoint Server 2016, 2019, and Subscription Edition, with an AI agent driving much of the discovery. Admins should confirm the July update that breaks the chain is installed, since Microsoft has not yet shipped the August fix.

Server prices to rise by up to 87% at OVHcloud (2 minute read)

OVHcloud is raising prices on its bare metal and public cloud servers by up to 87%, effective September 1 for new orders and October 1 for renewals, blaming AI-driven demand that has pushed up RAM and storage costs. Enterprise buyers face 59% increases on the latest High Grade AMD Epyc servers plus steep hikes on add-on memory and disks, though existing customers can lock in current pricing for up to four years by prepaying before October 1.

Cisco pushes urgent patches for actively exploited zero-day flaw crashing firewalls (1 minute read)

Cisco released emergency patches for an actively exploited zero-day in its firewall software that lets unauthenticated attackers crash devices remotely, with no workarounds available. IT teams running affected Cisco firewalls should treat patching as immediate, since the flaw is already being used in the wild and offers no mitigation short of the update.

Microsoft Patches 398 Flaws Including a Windows Driver Zero-Day Under Active Attack (1 minute read)

Microsoft's August Patch Tuesday closed 398 CVEs, including CVE-2026-68820, a use-after-free in the afd.sys kernel driver that is already under active attack to escalate local code to SYSTEM. IT teams should patch that zero-day first, then queue four unauthenticated RCE flaws rated CVSS 9.8 in Windows DNS Server, Deployment Services, QUIC, and HPC Pack, plus the SharePoint RCE fix pairing with July's auth-bypass patch.

Microsoft August 2026 Patch Tuesday fixes 400 flaws, 3 zero-days (1 minute read)

Microsoft's August 2026 Patch Tuesday fixed 400 vulnerabilities, including one actively exploited zero-day in the Windows Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock that grants SYSTEM privileges, plus 42 rated Critical. The volume reflects Microsoft's new AI-powered vulnerability discovery system, which it has warned will keep monthly patch counts elevated and raises the testing and deployment burden on enterprise IT teams already managing large Windows fleets.

IBM, Together AI team to offer open-source AI inference in the cloud (2 minute read)

IBM signed a $240M deal with neocloud provider Together AI to run a Nvidia HGX B300 cluster on IBM Cloud for large-capacity, open-source model inference. The arrangement gives IBM Cloud an enterprise-grade inference offering to compete with AWS, Azure, and CoreWeave, and positions Together AI's open-model platform, spanning DeepSeek, Nemotron, and Kimi, as a lower-cost alternative to single proprietary models for regulated enterprise workloads.

OVHcloud chief issues customer price warning amid surging hardware costs (1 minute read)

OVHcloud CEO Octave Klaba warned customers that prices for some Public Cloud and bare metal products could rise as much as 87%, driven by surging DDR5 RAM and storage costs he attributes to AI demand. The increases hit new orders in September and in-production equipment in October, following similar hikes from Hetzner, Alibaba, Tencent, and AWS, and signaling broader compute-cost inflation across enterprise cloud contracts.

Oracle set to bring quantum computing to OCI for hybrid AI (2 minute read)

Oracle will deploy Quantinuum's Helios quantum computer inside a US-based OCI AI data center and offer enterprise access through a planned OCI quantum service for hybrid quantum-AI and HPC workloads, with a preview expected in the coming months. Customers get managed, governed access to quantum hardware under existing OCI identity and security controls, though Forrester notes the integration lowers access barriers without accelerating real adoption timelines.

Microsoft reportedly raises Windows 11 license price significantly more than expected (1 minute read)

Microsoft has reportedly raised Windows 11 license prices sharply this year, an increase larger than industry observers anticipated, according to a new report. Enterprise buyers running volume licensing and OEM device fleets face higher per-seat costs at renewal, adding pressure to hardware refresh budgets already stretched by the Windows 10 end-of-support migration.

Zoom Annotation Flaws Could Let a Meeting Participant Hijack Another Attendee's Client (1 minute read)

Zoom patched three annotation-tool flaws that let any meeting participant execute code on other attendees' clients, including a zero-click buffer overflow reachable by a single malformed drawing sent from the wrong channel. Client fixes shipped in June and July across Zoom Workplace, VDI, Rooms, and the Meeting SDK, with no exploitation reported and none of the CVEs listed in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog.

Researchers Turn USB Auto-Install Into a Full SYSTEM Takeover on Windows 11 (1 minute read)

Researchers Alejandro Hernando and Borja Martinez showed at DEF CON 34 that Windows 11 Plug and Play auto-install can be chained into full SYSTEM code execution by an unprivileged user emulating trusted USB devices like Sierra Wireless and Sony FeliCa hardware. Microsoft notes the remote-over-RDP variant depends on USB redirection that is disabled by default, so exposure hinges on enterprise configuration rather than a default Windows flaw.

Metabase SQLi exploit grants attackers total access (2 minute read)

Metabase disclosed a critical zero-day SQL injection flaw (CVE-2026-72898, CVSS 10) in versions 1.58 and up, letting attackers gain raw SQL access to the BI platform's database and exfiltrate credentials, tokens, and API keys for connected systems like Snowflake, Databricks, and BigQuery. Metabase Cloud instances are already patched, but self-hosted deployments and OEM-embedded versions stay exposed until customers apply the fix, with Wiz finding Metabase in 13% of cloud environments.

SAP Commerce Cloud Flaw Could Let Unauthenticated Attackers Execute Arbitrary Code (1 minute read)

SAP released patches for CVE-2026-58231, a maximum-severity 10.0 CVSS flaw in Commerce Cloud's Data Hub Adapter that lets an unauthenticated attacker abuse a default authentication client to execute arbitrary code. The August update also fixes three other critical bugs across Manufacturing Integration and Intelligence and NetWeaver ABAP, so enterprise SAP teams should patch affected releases now and apply Onapsis's IP Filter Set workaround where redeployment lags.

Cisco warns of ASA and FTD VPN flaw exploited to crash devices (1 minute read)

Cisco warned that CVE-2026-20349, a high-severity flaw in Secure Firewall ASA and FTD software, is being actively exploited to remotely crash devices via crafted HTTP requests to the SSL VPN service. The unauthenticated denial-of-service bug carries an 8.6 severity score and has no workarounds, so IT teams running affected ASA 9.16 through 9.24 or FTD 7.0 through 10.0 releases must apply Cisco's hot fixes to remediate.

Malicious SIMs can shut down phones, steal files, and drag 5G back to 2G (1 minute read)

Researchers demonstrated that malicious SIM cards can silently shut down handsets, exfiltrate on-device files, and force 5G connections to downgrade to insecure 2G, exploiting the SIM Application Toolkit and over-the-air provisioning channels that carriers control. Enterprises running managed device fleets and cellular IoT deployments now have to treat the SIM itself as an untrusted component, adding supply-chain vetting and baseband monitoring to an attack surface most MDM stacks never covered.

Mozilla revokes Firefox signing key after unencrypted copy lands in GitHub (1 minute read)

Mozilla revoked a Firefox add-on signing key after discovering an unencrypted copy of it had been committed to a public GitHub repository. The exposure forced the organization to reissue signatures across its extension ecosystem, and enterprises that whitelist Firefox add-ons by signature should expect validation churn until the new key propagates through managed deployments.

Cisco Patches Firewall Zero-Day Exploited for DoS Attacks (1 minute read)

Cisco patched CVE-2026-20349, a zero-day in Secure Firewall ASA and FTD software that lets a remote, unauthenticated attacker crash appliances into a denial-of-service state via crafted HTTP requests to the Remote Access SSL VPN service. Cisco confirmed active exploitation in August, and CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog with a federal patch deadline of August 14.

Why observability doesn’t explain what happened (3 minute read)

Observability tools answer what is happening inside a system, but not why. The author argues that root cause usually lives outside the telemetry entirely, in a deploy from two days ago, a spiking support queue, or an approved change record. That gap between seeing and explaining now costs teams two to three hours of investigation per major incident, even when alerting and dashboards are well-tuned.
