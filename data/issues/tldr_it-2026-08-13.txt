Big Cloud is poised to corner the market for enterprise hardware (1 minute read)

The author argues that hyperscalers' massive AI-infrastructure spending is letting them corner the enterprise hardware supply chain, buying up GPUs, custom silicon, and server capacity at a scale that squeezes out traditional on-prem buyers. The consequence for IT leaders is thinner availability and worse pricing on the hardware they still run in their own data centers, accelerating the shift from owning infrastructure to renting it from AWS, Azure, and Google.

Salesforce and SAP are putting AI agents inside your workflows. Who tells them no? (2 minute read)

The author argues that Salesforce, SAP, and Oracle are embedding autonomous agents into the transactional core of CRM and ERP systems, letting software issue refunds, alter contract terms, and grant discounts without mapping those actions to the company's delegation-of-authority matrix. Gartner projects 80% of enterprise applications will ship these agents by 2026, yet most buyers vet licenses and security while never defining a signing threshold for what the agent can approve. The governance gap is procurement's problem, not the AI's.

The vendor consolidation trap: When one throat to choke costs more than it saves (3 minute read)

Vendor consolidation is sold as discipline: fewer vendors, simpler architecture, and volume pricing. The author argues the hidden cost is the competitive pressure it removes, since a shrunken vendor list lets the survivors price to your switching cost rather than the open market. The savings target books in year one, but leverage erodes at the first hard renewal, often after the business case's authors have moved on and left the CIO negotiating from the corner the program built.

What vibe-coding startup valuations portend for CIOs (3 minute read)

The author argues that soaring vibe-coding valuations, led by Lovable's $13.3B Series C, signal VCs betting these tools become a standing CIO line item rather than a developer niche. Unlike Cursor and Windsurf, which target engineers, Lovable and Replit ($9B) aim at business users, following the no-code path into the enterprise through chat-driven app prototyping. For CIOs, the shift moves app-building beyond IT, raising governance and shadow-IT questions as CEOs and customer-success staff ship working prototypes in hours.
