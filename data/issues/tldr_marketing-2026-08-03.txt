AI Search Data Is Now in Google Search Console: Here’s What You Need to Know (1 minute read)

Google launched dedicated generative AI performance reports in Search Console on June 3, 2026, giving site owners impression data for AI Overviews, AI Mode, and Discover AI features broken down by page, country, and device, with an opt-out toggle that carries no organic ranking penalty. The report omits click data and starts from May 18 with no backfill, so AI visibility is now measurable as a channel separate from traditional organic search.

Snapchat will no longer recommend AI slop (1 minute read)

Snapchat updated its Spotlight recommendation algorithm to demote wholly AI-generated videos, making "AI slop" ineligible for recommendation while human-made and AI-enhanced content still qualify. The move runs counter to Meta's Vibes app, which launched a fully AI-generated feed in February, and signals that platforms are now treating authentic, human-created content as a reach advantage rather than a constraint.

LinkedIn offers the option to report AI slop (1 minute read)

LinkedIn is testing an in-stream option that lets members report posts and comments as likely "AI slop," using the flags to train its detection classifiers and privately warn creators when their content reads as inauthentic. The move complicates LinkedIn's own generative-AI push, and it is replacing the "enhance your post" feature with a lighter proofreading tool as it tries to curb over-automated engagement.

What happened when 6.8m people were told real Monet art was AI (1 minute read)

When X user SHLOMS posted a real Monet painting and labeled it AI-generated, over 1,300 replies trashed it as "obvious AI slop," "incoherent muddle," and "garbage with no sense of space." The reaction maps to what ASU marketing professor Andrea Morales calls the "effort heuristic": audiences reward work they believe took time and punish anything that looks effortless, regardless of quality. For marketers deploying AI creative, the lesson is that perceived effort shapes reception as much as the output itself, so how you frame provenance matters.
