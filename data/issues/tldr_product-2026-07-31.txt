TBM 433: The Border Collie Faustian Bargain (1 minute read)

The author breaks down how people react when leadership presents a company strategy that experienced observers know isn't really a strategy, sorting the room into believers, savvy "players" who treat the kickoff as performance, the checked-out, seething purists, and the genuinely confused who wonder why no one just puts a real strategy on the slide. For PMs, the useful lens is recognizing which role you are playing in these rituals and deciding whether to keep nodding along or to name the gap.

The AI Productivity Paradox (1 minute read)

The author argues the "AI productivity paradox," where teams ship faster but outcomes stagnate, isn't a paradox at all. Most teams use AI to accelerate the same project model, which was always built to deliver output rather than outcomes, so faster building just means running the wrong direction faster. The takeaway for PMs is that AI widens the gap between strong product companies and everyone else, because the real advantage was always discovery, strategy, and culture, not delivery speed.

The Antithesis Principle (1 minute read)

The author argues that smart people reliably draw the obvious lesson from an observation while missing its inverted, self-directed one. If most people only learn from entertaining content, the obvious takeaway is to make your material engaging, but the wiser move is to become someone who can learn from a boring, unsexy book too. The same split applies to charisma: work on projecting it, but also train yourself to stop being swayed by it in others.
