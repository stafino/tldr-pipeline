Anthropic launches Claude Opus 5 with efficiency, safety improvements (1 minute read)

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5 across its chatbot and developer platform, positioning it as a cheaper model that approaches the quality of its unreleased flagship Mythos 5. Opus 5 beat the scaled-down Fable 5 on eight of 13 benchmarks at roughly 50% lower cost, and Anthropic calls it its safest model yet, citing a zero score on an internal test of vulnerability-exploitation ability.

Anthropic’s Claude Cowork could escape its local VM and read credentials on a Mac (1 minute read)

Security researchers at Accomplish AI showed that Anthropic's Claude Cowork could escape its local VM sandbox and read files across the host Mac, including SSH keys and cloud credentials, exposing roughly 500,000 macOS users before the issue was addressed. The SharedRoot attack chained a Linux kernel privilege-escalation bug (CVE-2026-46331) with a writable host filesystem mount, and Anthropic's fix defaults new Cowork sessions to cloud execution, leaving only local runs at risk.

Physicists turn particles in chaotic orbits into liquid computers — but this fluid hardware still trails memristor rivals (1 minute read)

Physicists at the Universities of Konstanz and Stuttgart built a reservoir computer from 400 microscopic silica particles orbiting in a drop of liquid, running chaotic-signal forecasting and anomaly detection with an F1 of 0.90 on the harder task. The colloidal array stayed roughly 10 times less accurate than memristor-based reservoirs, but held up when input reached only 20% of oscillators and when individual particles stopped responding or clumped together.

Open-source 3D-printed portable MRI machine built for under $70,000 — DIY medical equipment costs less than 7% of a full-sized MRI machine’s $1.1 million starting price (1 minute read)

The Open Source Imaging Initiative's OSI2 ONE scanner is a portable, 3D-printed MRI that can be built for under $70,000, roughly 7% of the $1.1 million starting price for a full-sized unit. Its 50mT field strength is far below the 1.5T to 3T of clinical machines, yielding lower resolution, but researchers are training AI on high-field scan data to denoise and reconstruct usable diagnostic images.
