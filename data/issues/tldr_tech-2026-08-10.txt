Tail-call optimization in C is relatively recent (1 minute read)

C compilers historically avoided tail-call optimization because the calling convention required the caller to clean up arguments after a call, forcing every call to be followed by a return and preventing the tail position from being reused. GCC gained a workaround with a separate calling convention in 2001 and now handles it, which makes copy-and-patch compilation with 100,000 code snippets practical where goto-based interpreter dispatch could not scale.
